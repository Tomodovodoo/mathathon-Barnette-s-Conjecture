# 27. Morse cancellation pairs the bad orientations

Fix the cyclic second state again. Its opposite-edge graph has one rootless cycle, so it has exactly two possible mates, corresponding to the two orientations of that cycle. The diagram pairs these two signed terms. More generally, k rootless cycles give a Boolean cube of mates, and reversing the first cycle pairs its vertices without fixed points. The empty cube at k equal to zero contributes a surviving tree state. This is the discrete Morse reading of fixed-state cancellation. It explains which terms disappear, but does not establish that a critical state exists somewhere. That requires the other regrouping and positive circulation. A separate Forman interpretation of the incidence collapse must leave the roots unpaired. The topology referee corrected that point and the direction of the J shift. The running example checks only a one-dimensional cube.
