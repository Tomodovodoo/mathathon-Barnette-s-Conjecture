Theorem 5.3. Let n ≥1, let t ∈A, and let s be a good state with inverse σ. Move x0 = s(t) to
t1 = altσ(x0) and take steps from the walk state S1 = (σ[x0 := t1], t1, x0). The walk stops, at the inverse
of a good state ιt(s). The map ιt is an involution on the set of good states and has no fixed point. So the
number of good states is even.
Proof. In S1 the excess is 2, the face t is empty and t1 has load 2. A step that does not stop keeps the
excess, so in every walk state reached t is the only empty face. The walk therefore stops exactly when it
moves a vertex into t, and then the excess is 0 and the result is a good state by Lemma 2.3.
The walk stops. Suppose S1 = step(S) for a walk state S. By Lemma 5.2, step(mir(S1)) = mir(S) is a
walk state. But the resident of mir(S1) is x0, and its exchange leads back into the empty face t, so this
step stops. Hence S1 is not in the image of step. An injective map on a finite set cannot return to an
earlier element of the forward orbit of an element outside its image. So the walk S1, S2, . . . ends at some
Sm whose step stops, with a good state s′.
Involution. Start from s′ at t. The vertex s′(t) is the resident of Sm, and its exchange returns
it to the face of Sm, so the first walk state is mir(Sm).
By Lemma 5.2 the walk continues with
mir(Sm−1), . . . , mir(S1), and the step from mir(S1) puts x0 into t and stops at s. So ιt(s′) = s.
No fixed point. If s′ = s, the two walks are the same, so Si = mir(Sm+1−i) for all i. For odd m the
middle state would equal its mirror, but the arrival and the resident differ. For even m the states Sm/2
and Sm/2+1 would have the same tree, but every step changes the tree.
□
Figure 4 shows one orbit. The proof uses no sign, no weight and no planarity beyond Lemma 5.1. It
does not give existence: if there is no good state, the statement is empty. With Theorem 1.1 it shows
that a triangulation with a pair has at least two good states.
