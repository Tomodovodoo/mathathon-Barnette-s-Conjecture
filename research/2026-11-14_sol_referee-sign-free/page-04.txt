For a rainbow tree σ, let parσ(x) be the black face whose centre is the parent of x in Fσ rooted at the
centre of t0.
Theorem 4.2.
(1) Every rainbow tree σ lies in exactly one 2-factor of B, namely σ ∪parσ.
(2) Inside a 2-factor F, the rainbow trees correspond to the sets of arcs of Gu that contain one arc
out of every vertex of X and no directed cycle; σ corresponds to the arcs x →u(σ′(x)). Such a
set is a spanning forest of Gu directed towards the roots. The good states inside F correspond to
the sets that contain exactly one of the two arcs of every face.
(3) Gu has a directed cycle if and only if B has a 2-factor other than F. If F is the only 2-factor, all
2n choices of σ inside F are rainbow trees and all states inside F are good.
Proof. (1) In Fσ the neighbours of x ∈X are the centres of its faces other than σ(x), all in A. So
parσ(x) ∈A and parσ(x) ̸= σ(x). The centre of t ∈A has 3 −cσ(t) neighbours, one of them its parent, so
t is the parent of 2 −cσ(t) vertices, none of them a root. Hence every t has degree cσ(t) + 2 −cσ(t) = 2
in σ ∪parσ, which is a 2-factor. If σ ∪σ′ is any 2-factor, Lemma 4.1 gives σ′ = parσ.
(2) The first sentence is Lemma 4.1. A set of n arcs with one arc out of every vertex of X and no
directed cycle leads from every vertex to a root. The arc from v through t is chosen exactly when σ′(v) = t,
so one arc in every face means that σ′ is a bijection, and then so is σ.
(3) Let v1 →v2 →· · · →vk →v1 be a directed cycle, the arc vi →vi+1 running through ti. Then
u(ti) = vi+1, so the ti are distinct, and replacing every edge (vi, ti) of F by (vi+1, ti) gives another 2-factor.
Conversely let F ′ ̸= F be a 2-factor. Every vertex meets as many edges of F \ F ′ as of F ′ \ F, so the
symmetric difference splits into closed trails that alternate between the two. An edge (v, t) of F ′ \ F has
v = u(t), so each trail is a closed directed walk in Gu, which contains a directed cycle. The last sentence
follows from (2).
□
