GOOD STATES WITHOUT SIGNS
Lemma 2.1 (duality). W is the plane dual of H, the edge e(t, v) being dual to the spoke (t, v). So for a
set S of spokes, H −S is a spanning tree of H if and only if {e(t, v) : (t, v) ∈S} is a spanning tree of W.
Proof. Let w be a white face with corners v1, v2, v3, and let ti be the black face across the side vivi+1.
The closed curve v1, c(t1), v2, c(t2), v3, c(t3) in H bounds w together with three thirds of black faces. This
region contains no vertex and no spoke, so it is a face of H. Different white faces give different faces, and
by Euler’s formula H has (3n + 3) −(2n + 4) + 2 = n + 1 faces, so these are all. The spoke (t, v) lies
between the two thirds of t at v, which belong to the faces around the white faces across the two sides of
t at v. The second sentence is the duality between spanning trees of a connected plane graph and of its
dual [3, Lemma 2.5].
□
Definition 2.2. A rainbow tree is a map σ: X →A with x a corner of σ(x) such that Fσ = H −
{(σ(x), x) : x ∈X} is a spanning tree of H. The load of t ∈A is cσ(t) = |σ−1(t)|, and the excess is
exc(σ) = P
t∈A |cσ(t) −1|.
In Fσ the centre of t keeps 3 −cσ(t) spokes and is not isolated, so every load is 0, 1 or 2. The excess is
even, and it is zero exactly when σ is a bijection.
Lemma 2.3. A state s is good if and only if its inverse σ = s−1 is a rainbow tree. So the good states are
the rainbow trees of excess 0.
Proof. In Fσ the centre of each t ∈A has degree two, and its neighbours are the ends of the side of t in
Qs. The centre of t0 is joined to the three roots. So Fσ is Qs with every edge subdivided once, plus one
new vertex joined to the roots. It has 2n + 3 edges on 2n + 4 vertices, so it is a spanning tree exactly
when it has no cycle, that is, when Qs is a forest in which no two roots share a component. A forest with
n edges on n + 3 vertices has three components, so this says that s is good.
□
By Lemmas 2.1 and 2.3, a good state is a spanning tree of W that satisfies two conditions. The face
condition asks for exactly one edge e(t, ·) for every black face t ∈A. The vertex condition asks for exactly
one edge e(·, x) for every x ∈X. A spanning tree of W has n edges, so either condition forbids the edges
at t0 and at the roots. A rainbow tree is a spanning tree of W with the vertex condition (Figure 1). A
spanning tree with the face condition is, by the proof of Lemma 2.3, a choice of one corner in every t ∈A,
not necessarily a bijection onto X, whose opposite sides form a forest with one root in each component.
