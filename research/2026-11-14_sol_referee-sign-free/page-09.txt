7. Where it stops
A cover with no good state. A good state inside a 2-factor F is a spanning tree made of edges of F
that meets both conditions. Corollary 6.10 meets each of them alone. Both together can fail.

of the 2-factors is a single cycle through all 13 vertices of X, and neither of its two states is good. It
contains 6144 rainbow trees, all of positive excess.

So no argument that works inside one cover can prove Theorem 1.1. Theorems 6.5 to 6.9 hold for every
deleted-corner map or every cover, the bad one included, and therefore cannot tell a cover that contains
a good state from one that does not. The signed proof moves between covers as well. Its cancellation
reverses a cycle of Qs, which changes the cover, and its second evaluation sums over all covers with the
fewest cycles. Here the move between covers is the exchange of Lemma 5.1 at a vertex whose other arc
would close a directed cycle in Gu.
The missing step.
Conjecture 7.2 (Step X). Let T have no separating triangle with black inner boundary. Then for every
rainbow tree σ there are rainbow trees σ = σ0, σ1, . . . , σk such that σi+1 arises from σi by one exchange,
exc(σi+1) ≤exc(σi) for all i, and exc(σk) = 0.
With Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 2.3 this implies Theorem 1.1. The conjecture has been checked by
exhaustive enumeration of all rainbow trees for every choice of t0 in every Eulerian triangulation with
at most 17 vertices, and in 1492 of the 1543 triangulations with 18 vertices; the other 51 exceeded the
time limit of the runs. In samples of larger triangulations, up to 120 vertices, every rainbow tree tried
could be brought to excess 0 by such a sequence. When B has only one 2-factor the conjecture holds by
Theorem 4.2(3): all choices inside the 2-factor are rainbow trees, and moving the second vertex of a face
of load 2 onwards along its cycle, face after face, reaches an empty face without raising the excess.
Three stronger statements would give a proof or an algorithm at once, and all three fail.
