GOOD STATES WITHOUT SIGNS
We use two theorems on packing. Nash-Williams [5], [8, Exercise 10.29]: the edges of a multigraph
without loops can be partitioned into k forests if and only if every nonempty set Z of vertices spans
at most k(|Z| −1) edges. Edmonds [2], [9, pp. 1674 and 1685–1686]: in a digraph with a vertex ϱ, the
largest number of pairwise arc-disjoint spanning trees directed towards ϱ equals the smallest number of
arcs leaving a nonempty set of vertices that does not contain ϱ. The source states this for trees directed
away from ϱ; reverse all arcs.
A deleted-corner map u chooses a corner u(t) of every t ∈A. Every 2-factor has one, but most
deleted-corner maps come from no 2-factor.

Theorem 6.8 (two forests in T). Suppose no separating triangle has black inner boundary, and let u be
a deleted-corner map. Then the 2n sides of the faces t ∈A at the corners u(t) can be partitioned into two
spanning forests of T, each with three components and one root in each component. For a pair (r, s) these
sides are Qr ∪Qs.
Proof. Identify the three roots to one vertex ϱ. The resulting multigraph T/R has n + 1 vertices, and the
2n sides give no loops, because an edge between two roots is a side of t0. A face t ∈A with jt corners in
a set has at most max(0, jt −1) of its two sides inside it. Let S be a nonempty set of vertices of T/R.
If ϱ /∈S, then by (6.1) and Lemma 6.3 the sides inside S number at most e(S) −b(S) ≤2|S| −2. If
ϱ ∈S, let U consist of S \ {ϱ} and the three roots, so |U| = |S| + 2; the sides inside number at most
e(U) −b(U) −2 ≤2|U| −6 = 2|S| −2. By the theorem of Nash-Williams the sides form two forests of
T/R, with 2n edges, hence two spanning trees. A spanning tree of T/R is a spanning forest of T with
one root in each of its three components. In a pair, the side of t opposite r(t) joins s(t) and u(t), and the
side opposite s(t) joins r(t) and u(t).
□
Theorem 6.9 (two rainbow trees in every cover). Suppose no separating triangle has black inner boundary.
Then every 2-factor F of B is the union of two rainbow trees σ and σ′.
Proof. Merge the roots of Gu into one vertex ϱ. By Lemma 4.1, a splitting F = σ ∪σ′ into two rainbow
trees is a partition of the arcs into two spanning trees directed towards ϱ. Conversely two such trees
without a common arc have n arcs each, one out of every vertex of X, so they use all arcs and give a
splitting. By the theorem of Edmonds they exist if at least two arcs leave every nonempty Y ⊆X. Every
vertex of Y has outdegree two, so this says that at most 2|Y | −2 arcs have both ends in Y . A face t has
such arcs only if u(t) ∈Y , and then at most jt −1 of them. By (6.1) and Lemma 6.3 with ρ(Y ) = 0 their
number is at most e(Y ) −b(Y ) ≤2|Y | −2.
□
Corollary 6.10. Suppose no separating triangle has black inner boundary, and let F be a 2-factor. Then
some spanning tree of W made of edges of F satisfies the vertex condition, and some other one satisfies
the face condition.
