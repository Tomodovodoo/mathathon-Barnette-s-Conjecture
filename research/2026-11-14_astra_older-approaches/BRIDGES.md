## 1. Feder–Subi and spanning trees of faces

LIT [FS, Section 2, Proposition 1 and Theorem 1; BF21, Definition 7]. Contract a facial 2-factor Q. A quasi spanning tree selects pairwise edge-disjoint faces D of R and proper vertices U such that the restricted radial graph R[U,D] is a tree. Each omitted, quasi vertex of degree 2r meets exactly r selected faces. FS's adjective "proper" is an additional restriction: every quasi vertex has degree four and no incident digon. BF21 calls vertices of U proper without imposing this additional FS adjective on the whole object. These usages must not be conflated.

LIT [FS, Theorem 1]. With blue faces Q and all other faces red, the theorem assumes red face lengths four or six. In its nonbipartite extension a blue triangle or pentagon must touch a red quadrilateral. In the bipartite three-face-coloured case this gives the familiar result with two colour classes restricted to four and six, and the third unrestricted. After contraction the red faces become digons and triangles. The constructive proof contracts triangular faces while controlling nested triangles and digons; its Lemma 1 preserves the invariant excluding separating digons and three children of one triangle. Its spanning-tree-parity algorithm for digons and triangles is a separate algorithmic result, not a proof that arbitrary prescribed constraints are always feasible. BF22 Sections 3–4 separate these feasibility and complexity issues.

PROVED-HERE. Here is a precise dictionary in the proper three-face-coloured setting. Let B be the blue vertices of T=G*, and let C be its other two vertex classes. Vertices of R correspond to B, faces of R to C, and their radial incidences are exactly the B–C edges of T. Contracting a blue primal face makes its original neighbours the incident faces at its contracted vertex. Each nonblue primal face loses alternating boundary edges, explaining the halving of its length. Thus this radial graph is T with the C–C edges removed, not O's black-triangle spoke graph H. The latter adds one centre per black triangle; it does not select a vertex colour class.

PROVED-HERE. Subject to the usual nondegenerate face-incidence convention, a quasi spanning tree gives a permeating induced tree S=U union D in T. Selected faces D being edge-disjoint says no C–C edge has both endpoints in D. B is independent. Consequently T[S] is exactly the radial tree. At a blue vertex outside U, selected neighbours cannot be consecutive around the vertex, and the half-degree condition forces strict alternation. Every triangle there meets S. Triangles at U also meet S. APSW Lemma 5 now gives the complementary induced tree and the Hamiltonian cycle. Conversely take a permeating induced tree S such that D=S intersect C is independent. Put U=S intersect B. At any blue vertex outside U, permeation forbids two consecutive unselected neighbours, while independence forbids two consecutive selected neighbours. Neighbours therefore alternate, giving the quasi degree condition. T[S] is the restricted radial tree. When every contracted vertex is incident with D, this is precisely the quasi spanning tree definition. FS's proper condition imposes the further degree-four/no-digon restriction. This proof exposes the extra colour restriction rather than suppressing it.

PROVED-HERE. This dictionary locates the older object at K12, the induced-tree partition, rather than identifying it with K8 or K10. A state deletes exactly one spoke at each bounded black centre and each nonroot vertex. A radial tree selects whole face nodes and all their incidences to U. A white cotree instead has one vertex per white triangle, with edges dual to deleted spokes. Their ground sets and constraints differ. No direct equality or general relaxation relation between these objects has been established. They have a common Hamiltonian consequence. A map through that consequence does not prove that every recovered cycle obeys FS's colour restriction.

REFUTED [test_routes.py; results.json]. The suggested equivalence between BF21 Theorem 11's parity and the black-inner obstruction is false as a literal condition. For G the truncated octahedron, take Q to be its six square faces. Then R is the octahedron, and every choice of an outer triangular face encloses three vertices. Theorem 11 explicitly includes the outer triangle, so its even-interior hypothesis fails. T=G* has no separating triangle at all. A pair exists and O's direct construction applies. The counterexample is a Goodey graph and satisfies the four/six condition on non-Q faces.

PROVED-HERE. The counterexample does not require trusting the enumeration. Truncating the octahedron produces six squares and eight hexagons; contracting the squares reverses truncation. The dual has one vertex for each square or hexagon. No two squares are adjacent. A triangle with one square corresponds to two consecutive faces around an original octahedral vertex and is facial. A triangle with no squares would be a triangle in the cube, the octahedron's face-adjacency graph, which is impossible. Thus every triangle of T is facial. In R the outer triangle leaves 6-3=3 interior vertices. BF21's parity condition and the absence of a black-inner separator therefore differ.

COMPUTED. On all 15 plantri Eulerian triangulations of orders 6 through 12, at all 262 facial roots, the flow-pair test agreed with absence of a black-inner separating triangle. Merely applying interior-vertex parity to separating triangles of the SAME T also disagreed with black-inner colour, in 78 rooted separator instances. This second comparison is diagnostic only; it is not the actual BF21 theorem. The contraction counterexample above is the correctly typed comparison.

LIT [O, Propositions 3.1 and 5.1, Sections 6–7]. O removes the face-size restriction by replacing the existence problem, not by extending FS's triangular contraction lemma. All constrained black faces of T have three corners regardless of primal face size. Pair existence follows from a planar density/flow argument, forest existence from disk-identity cancellation and nonvanishing, and separators are glued afterward. HUNCH. It is fair to call FS an earlier incidence-tree route to the same endpoint. It is not justified to say O is FS's parity argument completed.

## 2. Goodey, Kardoš and Schnieders

LIT [Goodey, result verified through FS and O]. Goodey's four/six theorem is an established predecessor. The original 1975 proof was not available in the downloaded corpus and was not obtained in this search. I therefore do not attribute a particular Kempe-switching or reduction mechanism to that proof from memory. This is a source gap, not evidence of absence.

LIT [K, Sections 2.4–2.5, Lemmas 5–6 and Theorem 4]. Kardoš constructs a simple odd perfect matching, where "odd" means its complementary 2-factor has an even number of components. White resonant hexagons produce a six-regular pseudograph which is the doubled-edge version of a cubic residual graph. A Payan–Sakarovitch independent-set/tree partition in that residual graph tells which resonant hexagons to flip. Replacing the three alternating 2-factor edges at each selected hexagon joins the result into one cycle. The difficult existence theorem builds an appropriate matching with cyclically four-edge-connected residual graph, handles exceptional nanotubes separately, and uses a computer-assisted patch analysis. Kardoš's local term "Barnette graph" has stronger face restrictions than the standard bipartite class; do not import his Lemma 2 as a statement that every Barnette graph has no cyclic three-cut.

PROVED-HERE. A primal resonant-face flip is not literally O's Q reversal. Q reversal keeps s fixed, while recovery of the final matching P and cycle depends only on s and the chosen root edge, so it leaves any defined recovered cycle unchanged. Indeed a Q reversal is used when s is cyclic and recovery is not defined. D reversal exchanges r and s around a union cycle in the incidence graph, preserving the undirected D and changing s. This is the usual alternating-matching exchange in a different graph; it has no supplied correspondence to a single primal resonant hexagon. A correspondence would need to prove that both endpoints admit recovery and identify the symmetric difference of their recovered matchings. Neither is part of the reversal definition. HUNCH. The common idea is joining components while controlling a plane tree, not an identification of moves.

LIT [S, Lemma 3.3, Sections 4–6]. Schnieders uses explicit graph substitutions preserving the boundary ports, enumerates external path pairings to certify lifting of Hamiltonian cycles, and proves that a reducible neighbourhood occurs using global face counts and structural cases. The stated computer count is 339,068,624. His connected bipartite theorem is stronger in connectivity than O's theorem on the bounded-face class. Section 6 identifies the difficulty at larger face sizes, including the need to preserve three-connectivity once connected counterexamples occur at face size ten. No state, disjoint mate, or signed paired-state sum appears in this mechanism. Boundary compatibility reappears in O's gluing, but O has only the three cap patterns rather than a catalogue of local patches.

COMPUTED [goodey.py]. All 18 Eulerian triangulations with degrees four/six and order at most 16 were tested at one documented root. Among those with pairs, c_min is one or two; two chosen roots have no pair. [refined.py] Refining every octahedral triangle into four yields a 32-vertex Goodey primal graph: 20 ordered pairs, four with one D-cycle, but all 12 tree pairs have three D-cycles. The analogous refined hexagonal bipyramid gives a 48-vertex Goodey graph: 112 pairs, 16 with one D-cycle, and all 56 tree pairs have three. Thus minimizing D-components is already the wrong forest constructor in the four/six class.

PROVED-HERE. There is no uniform bound on c_min for end-rooted Goodey duals when separating triangles are allowed. Take triangular layers L_0,...,L_k. Join each pair of successive layers as the two opposite triangular faces of an octahedron: vertex j of one layer joins vertices other than j of the next. Keep each layer's triangle. This is a chain of k octahedra, rooted at L_0. End vertices have degree four and internal-layer vertices degree six, so its dual is a Goodey graph. With L_0 black, the three black faces in each band have two corners on L_i and one on L_{i-1}. This follows first in the octahedron and then by matching colours across each cap. The first band's faces must match L_1 because L_0 is rooted. Once L_1,...,L_{i-1} are used, the next band's three faces must match L_i. In each band the possible incidences form a six-cycle, giving two states, and a disjoint mate must choose the other matching. Consequently D is exactly the union of the k layer triangles L_1,...,L_k, for every pair. Thus c_min=k. COMPUTED [chains.py] checks k=1 through 8, with 2^k states and 2^k ordered pairs. This does not settle a bound within the separator-free Goodey subclass.

PROVED-HERE. Ordinary fullerene graphs have pentagonal faces and are not bipartite; O's input hypothesis fails. They cannot be used as positive state-lab instances. Kardoš covers them by a different theorem. The four/six refined examples above provide valid hexagonal-lattice-like tests within O's domain. HUNCH. A canonical forest pair in the separator-free four/six subclass remains open here; no choice rule was found or asserted.

## 3. Stein and Alt–Payne–Schmidt–Wood

LIT [Stein, Theorem 2.2, pp. 218–219]. A B-set meets every region-cycle and contains none. Its complement has the same property. Stein obtains connected simply connected unions of regions via Alexander duality, whose common boundary is Hamiltonian; conversely a Hamiltonian Jordan curve supplies the two region classes. Region adjacency translates to vertex adjacency in T. This is the historical origin of the two induced trees, not a discovery of O.

LIT [APSW, Lemma 5, pp. 3–4]. A permeating subtree is induced and meets every face. Its complementary induced subgraph is acyclic by a separation argument, and Euler's formula forces exactly two components in their union, proving the complement connected. Contracting the two trees proves Hamiltonicity. The reverse direction uses the Hamiltonian Jordan curve and the count n-2. APSW Theorem 4 proves that any strategy forcing a colour class entirely in or entirely out of the permeating tree misses some Eulerian triangulations.

HUNCH, editorial judgment. APSW is the cleaner citation for the exact graph statement: it avoids invoking Alexander duality and names inducedness explicitly. Cite Stein for historical priority, APSW Lemma 5 for the equivalence, O Lemma 6.2 for the new construction of an acyclic selected-side set, and O Section 7 plus Kelmans/APSW for prescribed-pattern gluing. O's recovery proves a stronger construction guarantee from a forest state. The old equivalence alone supplies no such state. Its final Jordan-curve inducedness argument is an instance of the classical bridge.

## 4. Kelmans and Hertel

LIT [Hertel, Theorems 8–10, Corollary 3; APSW, Section 5, Theorem 8]. The literature already contains the global equivalence between Barnette and inclusion of any prescribed edge, avoidance of any prescribed edge, stronger facial prescriptions, and reduction to cyclically four-edge-connected graphs. These are class-level equivalences, not assertions that an arbitrary Hamiltonian cubic graph automatically has every strengthening. Hertel explicitly credits Kelmans with prior stronger results. Kelmans's original paper was not opened; use APSW's detailed proof exposition and state that provenance.

PROVED-HERE. O gives prescribed-edge inclusion directly. Let e=uv. Choose another incident edge f=uw. Apply O's construction with f* as the prescribed cap edge. The resulting Hamiltonian cycle avoids f. At u its degree must be two, so it uses both remaining edges, including e. This also forces any prescribed pair of incident edges. The argument uses O's construction and cubicity only, not a global equivalence theorem. It does not force an arbitrary length-three path: requirements at its two internal vertices need simultaneous avoidance at two locations.

COMPUTED [test_routes.py]. This direct forcing construction succeeded for all 138 edges of the four named graphs of orders 8,12,24,48. The output also records, edge by edge, inclusion frequencies across all recovered tree states for the first three graphs with a fixed root and fixed omitted edge. These are exact finite frequencies under uniform tree-state sampling, not a probability law asserted by O. In particular the prescribed omitted edge has frequency zero, whereas the two other edges at the root primal vertex have frequency one.

LIT [O Section 7; APSW Theorem 8]. Both routes must match which two cut edges the Hamiltonian cycle uses. In dual language the interface consists of a singleton tree label and a same-label edge. Gluing trees along one vertex or one edge preserves their being trees. This is the earlier separator strategy expressed with the new construction's boundary guarantee; it is not a novel decomposition theorem.

## 5. Pfaffian braces

LIT [GSW, Theorem 4.16]. Barnette, Hamiltonicity of planar cubic braces, P4-Hamiltonicity of planar cubic braces, P4-Hamiltonicity of cubic Pfaffian braces, and P4-Hamiltonicity or Hamiltonicity of cubic three-connected Pfaffian bipartite graphs are equivalent. GSW Corollary 4.9 builds cubic Pfaffian braces from planar cubic braces by cubic trisums, with the Heawood graph as the exceptional seed. Lemma 4.12 preserves prescribed three-edge paths across trisums. O Corollary 7.2 already applies this equivalence. Thus a non-Hamiltonian cubic three-connected Pfaffian bipartite graph would contradict the supplied proof and this published equivalence; the standard Horton/Georges–Kelmans nonplanar examples cannot be asserted Pfaffian.

COMPUTED [test_routes.py]. Heawood has 24 perfect matchings and 24 Hamiltonian cycles, with every tested three-edge path covered. The cubic trisum of three cubes is nonplanar, has 16 vertices, is two-extendable, has 36 perfect matchings and 12 Hamiltonian cycles, and also covers every three-edge path. K3,3 is a Hamiltonian two-extendable control with six cycles, but is not Pfaffian. Hamiltonicity and bracehood alone therefore do not certify the Pfaffian hypothesis. Pfaffianness of Heawood and the cube trisum is supplied by GSW's structural theorem, not tested by this program.

PROVED-HERE, bounded route audit. The literal planar pair proof cannot simply replace delta with a Pfaffian orientation. A Pfaffian orientation constrains conformal even cycles in the graph on which it is defined. O needs a separate incidence graph built from a black/white spherical triangulation, a matching count in each directed disk, and a common real weight field strictly positive around all counterclockwise spoke cycles. No transformation from a Pfaffian orientation of G to these objects is supplied. On an orientable surface of genus g, cubic duality gives |V(T)|=k+2-2g and hence |X|=k-1-2g after deleting three roots, whereas |A|=k-1. Even the balanced-state equation fails for the literal surface extension. Tree/cotree complementation also retains 2g extra edges. A replacement would need to address these failures before sign parity becomes relevant.

## 6. Tutte paths, Grinberg and reducible configurations

LIT [TY, Section 2, definitions and theorem 2.1]. A Tutte path or cycle bounds each bridge by three attachments, with at most two for bridges touching a specified boundary edge set. Four-connectivity removes any omitted component with such an attachment cut, yielding Hamiltonicity. Three-connectivity permits three-attachment components. Every cubic graph has vertex-connectivity at most three, so the standard four-connected conclusion cannot apply to G. Applying it to a four-connected dual gives a cycle in T, not one in G. Thomas–Yu extend the path/bridge framework and prove the projective-planar four-connected theorem and deletion-of-two-vertices planar theorem. O does not construct Tutte paths or enumerate their bridges. Its separator caps resemble a boundary interface, but they solve a different obligation. The original Tutte and Thomassen papers were not opened; TY states and uses Thomassen's theorem explicitly.

LIT [Grinberg, theorem; English translation]. A Hamiltonian plane cycle gives sum_f (|f|-2) on one side equal to the corresponding sum on the other. This is a necessary numerical test; a balanced face split alone provides no cycle. PROVED-HERE. O's disk count is related Euler bookkeeping, but the additional state equality B=I+l is a separate constraint absent from Grinberg's criterion. Combining it with B+W=2I+h-2 and 3(B-W)=2l-h forces 2l-h=-3. Its use is not to reject a proposed Hamiltonian cycle but to give the exact phase of every directed state cycle. Thus Grinberg supplies a useful explanatory analogy, not the signed cancellation or its existence conclusion.

LIT [BGM, Sections 2 and 4; HMM through BGM]. Reducible-configuration approaches prove that a minimal counterexample must avoid certain configurations, reduce the surviving generation space, and use Hamiltonicity tests plus cycle-lifting arguments. HMM reached 64 vertices; BGM reaches 90 for Barnette graphs. The obstruction to a general theorem is that the finite census does not exclude all larger reduced candidates. O contains no HMM expansion enumeration or bounded-size search. Its unbounded pair-existence and cancellation statements supply a different existence proof rather than extending the census. The recurring object is a compatible path pattern across a cut, also made explicit in Schnieders's substitution certificates and the categorical route notes. HMM's original paper was unavailable, so no original lemma number or detailed generation claim is attributed to it here.

## 7. Trinity before 2026

LIT [HK, Sections 1–2, Theorem 4.3 and Lemma 4.4]. O's states are Tutte matchings with the face colours exchanged. Tutte's tree-trinity theorem equates arborescence counts; Hine–Kálmán studies state transitions and lattice structure, including the different behaviour on the torus. These are single-state results. LIT [Kalman, Theorem 9.10, pp. 865–866]. Berman's determinant of the nonroot vertex/nonouter triangle adjacency matrix counts states because its nonzero determinant terms have a common sign. This directly predates state-counting and determinant descriptions in the later expositions.

HUNCH, bounded historical conclusion. Targeted searches for "tree trinity" with "Hamiltonian", "Tutte matchings" with "Hamiltonicity", and "Tutte matching" with "Barnette", plus a local search and reading of HK and the downloaded Kálmán papers, found no pre-2026 theorem using paired Tutte states to prove Barnette or Hamiltonicity. This is not a proof that no such connection exists. Tutte 1975 and Berman 1980 originals were not obtained; their mathematical content was checked through HK and Kálmán, respectively. Their complete bibliographies and adjacent knot-theory literature remain a priority check before claiming "first".

PROVED-HERE. The precise missing old-to-new obligation is not existence of a Tutte matching. It is existence of one whose deleted spokes leave a tree, together with a disjoint mate enabling cancellation. A single-state clock theorem does not say that the subset with a disjoint mate is preserved, nor that it contains a forest state. O's K3–K7 address these additional predicates. This is where the novelty statement should focus.
