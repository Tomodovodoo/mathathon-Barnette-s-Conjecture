                                                  Abstract. Barnette’s conjecture asserts that every cubic 3-connected plane bipartite graph
                                                  is hamiltonian. Although, in general, the problem is still open, some partial results are known.
                                                  In particular, let us call a face of a plane graph big (small) if it has at least six edges (it has
                                                  four edges, respectively). Goodey proved for a 3-connected bipartite cubic plane graph P ,
                                                  that if all big faces in P have exactly six edges, then P is hamiltonian. In this paper we prove
                                                  that the same is true under the condition that no face in P has more than four big neighbours.
                                                  We also prove, that if each vertex in P is incident both

                                                                                       1. Introduction
                                             In this paper we consider only finite and simple graphs. We use Diestel [2] as a reference
                                          for undefined terms. In particular, if G is a plane graph, then V (G) is the set of vertices and
                                          ∆(G) (or δ(G)) is the maximum (minimum, respectively) degree of G. For U ⊆ V (G), the
                                          neighbours in V (G) \ U of vertices in U are called neighbours of U .
                                             Let P denote the family of all cubic 3-connected plane bipartite graphs. In 1969, Barnette
                                          (see Tutte [17], Problem 5) conjectured that every graph of P has a Hamilton cycle. The
                                          problem whether a cubic bipartite plane graph has a Hamilton cycle (without the assumption
                                          of 3-connectivity) is NP-complete, as shown by Takanori, Takao and Nobuji [16]. Therefore,
                                          it seems wise to discuss only sufficient conditions for a graph of P to be hamiltonian. Goodey
                                          [7] proved that if a graph in P has only faces with 4 or 6 edges, then it is hamiltonian.
                                          Kelmans [12, 13] proved that Barnette’s conjecture holds if and only if every cyclically-4-edge-
                                          connected graph of P has a Hamilton cycle (a graph is cyclically-k-edge connected if it has no
                                          edge cut of size k − 1 such that both sides of the cut contain a cycle). Brinkmann, Goedgebeur
                                          and McKay [3] (see also Holton, Manvel and McKay [10]) used computer search to confirm
                                          Barnette’s conjecture for graphs up to 90 vertices. Florek [4] proved that if a graph of P
                                          possess a 2-factor consisting only of facial 4-cycles, then it has a Hamilton cycle.
                                             Stein [15] expresses hamiltonicity in terms of the dual graph (see also S.L. Hakimi and E.F.
                                          Schmeichel [8]). Let H be a plane triangulation. Stein proved that the dual H ∗ has a Hamilton
                                          cycle if and only if there exists a partitioning of the vertex set of H into two subsets each of
                                          which induces a forest. Notice that G∗ ∈ P if and only if G is an Eulerian plane triangulation
                                          different from 3-cycle. In view of the Stein theorem Barnette’s conjecture is equivalent to the
                                          problem whether for each Eulerian plane triangulation there exists a partitioning of V (G) into
                                          two subsets each of which induces a forest.

                                             Key words and phrases. Barnette’s conjecture, Eulerian plane triangulations, three colourable plane graphs,
                                          vertex partition into two induced forests.

   A k-colouring of a graph G is a mapping c : V (G) → S, where S is a set of k colours.
Alternatively, a k-colouring may be viewed as a partition {Vi : i ∈ S} of V (G), where Vi
denotes the (possibly empty) set of vertices assigned to colour i. A colouring c is proper if no
two adjacent vertices are assigned the same colour. Then, each Vi forms an independent set
of vertices. A graph is k-colourable if it has a proper k-colouring. Note that every Eulerian
plane triangulation has a unique proper 3-colouring (see Heawood [9]).
   Let G be an Eulerian plane triangulation different from 3-cycle. A vertex of V (G) is called
big (small) if it is of degree at least 6 (of degree equals 4, respectively). Let B denote the set
of all big vertices in G and suppose that G[B] is a subgraph of G induced by the set B. If
C is a cycle in G whose vertices are all small in G, then there are tree possibilities: G is the
octahedron, or C is of even order and G is the join C ∨ E 2 (where E 2 is an empty graph of
order 2), or C is a facial 3-cycle such that neighbours of V (C) induce a facial 3-cycle in G[B].
If G 6= C 2l ∨ E 2 , for l ⩾ 2, then each path P in G in which all inner vertices are small and
ends are big is either an induced path in G or P + ac is an induced cycle in G, where a, c
are different adjacent ends of P . Notice that the set V0 (P ) of all neighbours of V (P ) consists
of two big vertices in G (say b, d) and abcda is a facial 4-cycle in G[B]. An easy verification
shows that the following conditions are equivalent:
    (1) G 6= C 2l ∨ E 2 , for l ⩾ 2,
    (2) G has at least three big vertices,
    (3) every facial cycle in G[B] is a 3-cycle or a 4-cycle,
    (4) G[B] is 2-connected.
Let Θ be the family of all 2-connected 3-colourable plane graphs having only faces bounded
by 3-cycles or 4-cycles. By the equivalence of conditions (2) − (4), if G is an Eulerian plane
triangulation with at least three big vertices, then G[B] ∈ Θ.
   Let J ∈ Θ and suppose that J = {J1 , J2 , J3 } is a partition of V (J), where each Ji is a
(possibly empty) independent set in J.
Definition 1.1. A partition K of V (J) into two subsets is called compatible with J if every
facial 4-cycle in J has two non-consecutive vertices which belong either to the same part of K
and J or to distinct parts of K and J .
   The following lemma asserts that using the partition J we can obtain many partitions of
V (J) into two subsets which are compatible with J . We prove Lemma 1.2 in Chapter 3.
Lemma 1.2. If I ⊆ J1 ∪ J2 is an independent set in J, then the partition {(J1 ∪ J2 ) \ I, J3 ∪ I}
of V (J) is compatible with J .
   Let G be an Eulerian plane triangulation with at least three big vertices and suppose
that {V1 , V2 , V3 } is the unique partition of V (G) into three independent sets in G. Then
B = {B ∩ V1 , B ∩ V2 , B ∩ V3 } is a partition of B into three independent sets in G[B]. Notice

Theorem 1.6. Let J ∈ Θ with ∆(J) ⩽ 4 and suppose that J is a partition of V (J) into three

  If G is an Eulerian plane triangulation different from 3-cycle, then, by the equivalence of
conditions (1) − (2), G = C 2l ∨ E 2 , for some l ⩾ 2, or G has at least three big vertices.
Certainly, if G = C 2l ∨ E 2 , then G∗ is hamiltonian. Recall that if G has at least three big
vertices, then G[B] ∈ Θ. Hence, from Theorem 1.6 and Theorem 1.3, we obtain immediately
the following main result.
Theorem 1.7. Let G be an Eulerian plane triangulation different from 3-cycle. Then G∗ is
hamiltonian if no vertex in G has more than four big neighbours.
    Jensen and Toft ([11], Problem 2.12) showed that Barnette’s conjecture is true if and only
if for every 3-colourable plane graph H there exists a vertex partition into two subsets each of
which induces a forest in H. This is a consequence of the Stein [15] result and the following
Król [14] result: a plane graph is 3-colourable if and only if it is a subgraph of some Eulerian
plane triangulation. Chen, Raspaud and Wang [1] proved that for every plane graph without
intersecting 3-cycles there exists such partition. In the following Proposition 1.8 we show that
Barnette’s conjecture is connected with the family Θ. Its proof is contained in Chapter 3.
Proposition 1.8. Barnette’s conjecture is true if and only if for every J ∈ Θ and for each
partition J of V (J) into three independent subsets (possibly one of which is empty) there exists
a partition K of V (J) into two subsets which is compatible with J and each part of K induces
a forest in J.

Corollary 2.2. If each 3-cycle in G has both a small and a big vertex, then B(G) is bipartite

all big vertices of V (G). If λ is a facial 3-cycle in G[B], then it is not a facial 3-cycle in G,

Therefore G[B] is bipartite.

that each facial cycle in G[B] has two pairs of non-consecutive vertices belonging to the same
class of L. Hence, L is compatible with B. In view of Theorem 1.3, G∗ is hamiltonian.           

Definition 4.2. Let t : V (J) → {α, β, γ} be a colouring of V (J). A subgraph of J is called

Definition 4.3. We say that a colouring t : V (J) → {α, β, γ} (non proper) is associated with
