3. Corner choices and the tree of faces
Fix a face t0 of T and one of its sides e0 = ab, and let o be the third corner of t0. Exchanging the
colours if necessary, t0 is black; selections do not depend on the colours. We draw t0 as the outer face, so
that “inside” a cycle means on the side away from t0. The corners a, b, o are the roots. Let A be the set of
black faces other than t0 and X the set of vertices other than the roots. By (2.3), |A| = |X| = k −1 =: n.
Where the corner choices come from. Suppose P is an acyclic selection containing e0, and put
P0 = P \ {e0}.
Lemma 3.1. Each of the three components of P0 contains exactly one root. Orient every edge of P0
towards the root of its component, and for t ∈A let s(t) be the tail of the side of t in P0. Then s is a
bijection from A to X, and s(t) is a corner of t for every t.
Proof. By Proposition 2.6(2), a and b lie in one component of P and o in the other, because ao /∈P.
Deleting e0 splits the first component into a tree containing a and a tree containing b. In a tree oriented
towards its root every vertex other than the root has exactly one outgoing edge, so every vertex of X is
the tail of exactly one edge of P0, and the roots are tails of none. The side of t ∈A in P is not e0, which
lies in t0, so s(t) ∈X is defined. Distinct black faces have distinct selected sides, because an edge has
only one black face, and distinct edges of P0 have distinct tails, so s is injective; it is onto because every
x ∈X is the tail of an edge of P0, whose black face lies in A.
□
So the unknown is a choice of one corner in every face of A that uses every vertex of X once.
Definition 3.2. A state is a bijection s: A →X such that s(t) is a corner of t for every t ∈A.
We will build P from a state, and P will contain, in every t ∈A, one of the two sides of t at s(t). The
third side of t, opposite s(t), is excluded (Figure 3). Let Qs be the set of these opposite sides, one in
each face of A; they are distinct because an edge has only one black face, so |Qs| = n. In t0 the selection
must contain e0 and exclude ao and bo. Put Ss = Qs ∪{ao, bo}, the set of edges that every such selection
excludes; it has n + 2 = |V (T)| −1 elements.
