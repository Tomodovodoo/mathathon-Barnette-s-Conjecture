2. The dual triangulation and the target
The dual. Fix an embedding of G in the sphere, with edges drawn as polygonal arcs. Its dual T has a
vertex in each face of G and an edge crossing each edge of G once. Conversely G is the graph of faces of
T: each face of T contains one vertex of G, and two faces are adjacent in G when they share a side.
Lemma 2.1. Let G be as in Theorem 1.1. Then T is a simple graph with at least four vertices, every
face of T is bounded by a triangle, and the faces of T can be coloured black and white so that every edge
of T has one black and one white face.
Proof. First, G has no set of at most two edges whose removal separates a nonempty vertex set U from
the nonempty rest. If |U| = 1 then three edges leave U, and if |U| = 2 at least four do, since G is simple.
So |U| ≥3, and likewise for the rest. Deleting the at most two endpoints in U of the cut edges leaves a
nonempty part of U with no edge to the rest, contradicting 3-connectivity.
A loop of T, or two edges of T joining the same two vertices, is a closed curve that meets G in one
or two points, on one or two edges of G. The two ends of a crossed edge lie on opposite sides of the
curve, so both sides contain vertices of G, and the crossed edges form a cut of size at most two. Hence
T is simple. The face of T containing a vertex v of G is bounded by a closed walk through the three
faces of G at v, and in a simple graph a closed walk of length three is a triangle. Euler’s formula gives
|V (T)| = |F(G)| = 2 + |E(G)| −|V (G)| = 2 + |V (G)|/2 ≥4. Finally, colour the face of T containing v by
the class of v in the bipartition of G. The two faces at an edge of T contain the two ends of an edge of G,
so they get different colours.
□
