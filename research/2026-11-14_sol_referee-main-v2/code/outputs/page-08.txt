Euler’s formula needs a connected plane graph, so we first say what the inside of Z is. Let RZ consist of
Z and the vertices and edges of T inside Z. By the Jordan curve theorem the outside of Z is a connected
open region with boundary Z; it contains no point of RZ, so it is one face of RZ, bounded by the cycle
Z. Every other face of RZ lies inside Z and contains no vertex or edge of T, so it lies in one face of T;
conversely a face of T inside Z is a connected region disjoint from RZ whose boundary triangle lies in RZ.
So the faces of RZ are the B + W faces of T inside Z and the outside of Z, each bounded by a cycle, and
RZ is connected by Lemma 2.2. We also use that every face at a vertex strictly inside Z lies inside Z.
Lemma 3.8 (disk identity). Let r be a state and Z a directed cycle of chosen sides for r, of length h.
Let B and W be the numbers of black and white faces inside Z, and ℓthe number of edges of Z whose
black face is inside. Then W = B + 1 and h = 2ℓ+ 3. In particular h is odd and Z encloses at least one
white face.
Proof. Two counts hold for every cycle of T. The plane graph RZ has I + h vertices, B + W + 1 faces,
and (3(B + W) + h)/2 edges, since each inner face has three sides, an edge strictly inside lies on two inner
faces and an edge of Z on one; Euler’s formula gives the first count below. For the second, count each
side of an inner face with +1 if the face is black and −1 if it is white: an edge strictly inside contributes
+1 −1, and an edge of Z contributes +1 or −1 according to its inner face. Thus
B + W = 2I + h −2,
3(B −W) = 2ℓ−h.
The third count uses the state. Send each black face t inside Z to r(t), a vertex inside or on Z. This
is injective because r is. Every vertex v strictly inside is hit: v = r(t) for some t ∈A because r is onto
X, and t has the corner v, so it lies inside. A vertex v on Z is r(t) for exactly one t, and the edge of Z
leaving v is the chosen side of that t, because each face has only one chosen side and r is injective. So v
is hit exactly when that edge has its black face inside. This happens for ℓvertices of Z, so
B = I + ℓ.
Substituting into the first count gives W = I + h −ℓ−2, hence B −W = 2ℓ−h + 2, and the second
count becomes 3(2ℓ−h + 2) = 2ℓ−h, that is h = 2ℓ+ 3. Then W −B = h −2ℓ−2 = 1.
□
