History and sources. We follow Hertel’s survey [9] for the older history. Tait conjectured in 1884 that
cubic 3-connected planar graphs are Hamiltonian, and Tutte disproved this in 1946 with a non-bipartite
example [17]. Barnette asked whether bipartiteness rescues the statement. Before 2026 the conjecture
was known when all faces have four or six sides [9], under conditions on two of the three face colour
classes [5], and when all faces have at most eight sides [15]. Computer checks reached 64 vertices in work
of Holton, Manvel and McKay [9], and 90 vertices in work of Brinkmann, Goedgebeur and McKay [3]. In
a preprint dated 24 September 2026, OpenAI proved Theorem 1.1 [14]. The paired-state argument, disk
identity, flow argument and induction are theirs. States are the corner choices of Tutte’s theory of trinity,
as explained by Hine and Kálmán [10]. The target, two induced trees in T, is Stein’s reformulation [16]
in the form of Alt, Payne, Schmidt and Wood [2, Lemma 5]. Two expositions followed, one by Li [12],
who works in G, and one in an unsigned manuscript [4], which adds an exact criterion for the first step
and an enumeration algorithm. Remark 4.10 and Section 7 say how these accounts and ours differ.
