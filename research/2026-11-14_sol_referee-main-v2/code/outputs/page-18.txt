BARNETTE’S CONJECTURE
7. Remarks
How this account differs from the sources. The proof is that of [14], and its presentation here
differs in four places. The disk identity is stated as “one more white face than black”, Lemma 2.2 makes
Euler’s formula applicable to the inside of a cycle, and Proposition 3.9 reads the acyclicity of the selection
off that statement. The sign of a pair is real, as in [4], and is written as a product of local signs. The
weights are heights read along paths of faces. They are the weights that OpenAI construct in the proof
of [14, Lemma 5.3] for one choice of their circulations, and they are Li’s counts of crossings between
half-rays and matching edges [12, Section 4] taken along fixed paths. This choice removes the spokes and
the sliding argument of [14, Section 5.3], and the finite difference of Lemma 4.7 replaces the exponential
sum. The tree of faces Ys is obtained from the opposite sides by planar duality in T; in G it is Li’s black
dual trace [12, Section 6] with one extra leaf.
Which Hamiltonian cycles arise. For fixed t0 and e0, Lemma 3.1 assigns a state sC to every
Hamiltonian cycle C avoiding the edge across e0. C is produced by Proposition 3.9 exactly when sC
is good, and different good states give different cycles. If sC is good, the complement P of C avoids
SsC, because its side in each t ∈A contains sC(t) and its side in t0 is e0. Thus P and PsC are perfect
matchings of the tree YsC, and a tree has at most one perfect matching, so P = PsC. Conversely, when C
comes from a good state s, directing Ps \ {e0} out of s(t) gives outdegree one at each nonroot and zero at
each root. On each rooted tree this is its orientation towards the root, so Lemma 3.1 recovers sC = s.
This also proves that different good states give different cycles. In the example all six such cycles arise.
In the hexagonal prism, whose dual is the hexagonal bipyramid, take a black face as t0 and its side on
the equator as e0; there are three states, two of them good, and four Hamiltonian cycles avoiding the
edge, so only two arise. For the two other sides of t0 there are two such cycles, and both arise.
Why each hypothesis is needed. Bipartite. The colouring of the faces of T is the bipartition of G. It
gives every edge one black face, which makes Qs and D(r, s) consist of distinct edges and gives |A| = |X|.
Tutte’s 46-vertex graph [17] is cubic, planar and 3-connected but not Hamiltonian. The smallest such
graphs have 38 vertices [11].
Planar. On an orientable surface of genus g the same counting gives |V | = k +2−2g, so |X| = |A|−2g
and there are no states when g > 0. The smallest non-Hamiltonian cubic bipartite 3-connected graph is
the 50-vertex graph of Georges and Kelmans [7, 3].
Cubic. Cubicity makes the faces of T triangles, so that a corner has one opposite side, and it gives the
complement of a selection degree two. The incidence graph of the vertices and faces of the triangular
bipyramid has classes of sizes 5 and 6, so it has 11 vertices and no Hamiltonian cycle. It is planar by
drawing each face centre joined to its three corners. Here is also a check of 3-connectivity. The bipyramid
has equatorial vertices p1, p2, p3, apices u, v, and faces up1p2, up2p3, up3p1, vp1p2, vp2p3, vp3p1. Deleting
at most two of its five vertices leaves a connected graph, as is seen directly from the equatorial triangle
and the two apices. If at most one face centre is deleted from the incidence graph, every surviving
bipyramid edge is still represented by a two-edge path through one of its incident face centres, and every
remaining face centre has a surviving corner. If two face centres are deleted, no original vertex is deleted,
and at most one bipyramid edge loses both its incident face centres, since two faces share at most one
side. The bipyramid stays connected after deleting one edge, and the surviving face centres all attach to
it. Thus the incidence graph stays connected after any two vertex deletions.
3-connected. It is used once, to make T simple (Lemma 2.1). Simplicity is used in the density bound,
where a face of H of length two would break bal(Ψ) ≤dΨ −4, and in the pieces of Section 6. Take two new
vertices and three copies of the cube with one edge removed, and join the two ends of the removed edge
in each copy to the two new vertices, one to each, respecting the bipartition. The result is cubic, bipartite
and planar with 26 vertices. To check 2-connectivity, each cube with an edge removed is 2-connected and
joins the two new vertices by a path. Deleting a new vertex leaves the three copies connected through the
other new vertex. Deleting a vertex of a copy leaves that copy connected to at least one new vertex, while
the other two copies still connect the new vertices. Deleting both new vertices leaves three components,
so the graph is 2-connected but has no Hamiltonian cycle. Deciding Hamiltonicity is NP-complete for
2-connected cubic bipartite planar graphs [1].
No separating triangle. Proposition 5.2 uses it once, and it is not necessary: by [4, Theorem 1.2], a
pair exists exactly when no separating triangle has black faces along all three of its sides on the side away
from t0. Two octahedra glued along a face (9 vertices) give the smallest examples, with a pair for one
choice of t0 and none for another of the same colour; an exhaustive check over every face rooting of every
Eulerian sphere triangulation with at most 12 vertices confirms this minimality.
