Since ηs(y) is a sum over faces, and different cycles of D(r, s) use different faces, the effects of reversing
several cycles add up.
The total is not zero. It remains to choose F. Call the undirected edge set of D(r, s) the cover of the
pair; OpenAI call it a group. Start with a single cycle. On the cube the two states of Figure 1(f) give two
pairs, one in each order, with the same cover: the triangle 3, 4, 5 around one white face. Reversing the
triangle turns one pair into the other, so by Lemmas 4.4 and 4.6 the two pairs have opposite signs and
weights that differ by 1. With F = 1 the two terms cancel, and with F = ω they leave ±1.
A cover with c cycles carries 2c pairs, one for each choice of orientations, as the proof of Proposition 4.8
shows. Each reversal flips the sign, and the weight is a constant plus L(C) for each cycle C that runs
counterclockwise. So the signed sum of F(s) over the cover is a difference taken once along each of the c
cycles. For a power of ω the next lemma computes it. A c-fold difference kills every power below c, and
on the c-th power it leaves ±c! times the product of the steps L(C), each at least 1. So we take F = ωm,
where m ≥1 is the smallest number of cycles of a cover, assuming a pair exists. Covers with more than
m cycles then contribute nothing, and the covers with exactly m cycles contribute nonzero products of
one sign. Figure 11 shows this for the two covers of the example.
