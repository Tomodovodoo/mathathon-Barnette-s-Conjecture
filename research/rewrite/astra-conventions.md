The standard comparison is [Alt–Payne–Schmidt–Wood, Lemma 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3783): Hamiltonicity is equivalent to partitioning the dual vertices into two induced trees. Eulerian means even vertex degrees, not an Eulerian orientation. Batagelj’s inductive triangulation constructions and Brinkmann–McKay’s plantri concern this standard embedded class; they do not supply names for the new signed-count objects.

For prescribed outdegrees, use *alpha-orientation* only for orientations of a fixed graph with specified outdegree function alpha. A state alone is not such an orientation. Felsner’s lattice theorem and Propp’s orientation lattices do not automatically apply as Q_s changes with s. Nor is the three-root construction a Schnyder wood: the required colours and local rotation rules have not been supplied. Tutte’s trinity and Hine–Kálmán justify “state”. [Kenyon–Propp–Wilson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9903025) justify comparing incidence matchings and rooted trees with Temperley’s bijection, not identifying these constructions without proof. A pair of disjoint matchings resembles a double-dimer configuration, but excludes doubled edges. Kasteleyn theory concerns Pfaffian/determinantal signs; it does not identify epsilon with a matching permutation sign.

Call the cancellation a *sign-reversing involution*, as in Stanley, Zeilberger and Lindström–Gessel–Viennot arguments; prove preservation of the domain and weight, reversal of sign, and involutivity. The fixed cycle must depend only on the data preserved by the map. Do not call it LGV without a path-determinant model, or Thomason’s lollipop argument without Hamiltonian-path rotations and its parity graph. Diestel and Bondy–Murty are appropriate defaults for graph terminology, Mohar–Thomassen for embeddings and duality, Lovász–Plummer for matching terminology. These comparisons do not establish an application of their theorems.

## Statements and page design

Concrete models are [Alt et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3783), [Thomas–Yu, JCTB 62](https://thomas.math.gatech.edu/PAP/ham.pdf), and [Bagheri Gh.–Feder–Fleischner–Subi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.05483). Alt states the conjecture early and uses ordinary numbered lemmas; Thomas–Yu gives compact numbered assertions, introduces terminology after the results, and announces the proof sections in one paragraph. Bagheri et al. illustrates why “spanning tree of faces” should be reserved for its expressly defined embedding-dependent meaning. These papers offer models, not a uniform journal mandate.

Use Theorem for the main result and strengthened dual statement, Proposition for the two substantial intermediate results, Lemma for reusable proof steps, and Claim inside one proof. An Observation should be immediate; do not promote every sentence to one. Most lemmas need no title. In LaTeX an optional argument such as `\begin{lemma}[Disk identity]` normally prints parenthetical attribution or a short name, not literal brackets. Prefer a citation or precise descriptor over “Key Lemma”. Define short terms inline in italics. Reserve a Definition environment for a substantial condition needing later reference.

Declare finite simple graphs and the embedding convention once. State the main theorem independently. Begin the technical section with the standing triangulation, colouring and roots; repeat only hypotheses that vary. Warn explicitly when intermediate duals allow parallel edges. Induct on |V(T)|: dispose of the case without separating triangles by the preceding proposition, cut along Delta, verify both pieces satisfy the hypotheses and are smaller, choose compatible prescribed edges, then prove the glued object has the required incidences and is acyclic. Two short Claims may isolate these last checks. Avoid a separate subsection for every sentence.

Recommend three schematic figures: primal/dual matching, the two reversals, and gluing across Delta. Place each near first use with ordinary floats, not `[H]`; this is an editorial recommendation, not a journal quota. Remove enumeration tables and repeated large examples from the concise paper. Aim for an abstract of 100–150 words: attribution, theorem, and mechanism. Introduce the theorem promptly, give one history paragraph, explain the two groupings of the signed sum, and finish with one organisation sentence. Do not define elementary connectivity or teach Hall’s theorem in the introduction.

With amsart and amsplain, use numbered citations and the generated alphabetically arranged bibliography; give theorem locators in optional citation arguments. Remove internal audit notes from publishable entries. Cite manuscripts as unpublished manuscripts with author, exact title, date and stable URL if available. For the unsigned manuscript omit author rather than inventing authorship. Cite software separately with version or commit and persistent URL; distinguish a Lean development from a fully checked formalization of the stated theorem. Existing OpenAI2026, Li2026, Anonymous2026 and OpenAI2026b cover the supplied sources.

Suggested title: *A proof of Barnette’s conjecture, after OpenAI*. First sentence: “We give a self-contained exposition of the proof of Barnette’s conjecture in [citation].” Credit Li’s reformulation and the unsigned manuscript where used. A title footnote may explain provenance, but cannot replace citations at adapted arguments. MSC 2020: primary 05C45; secondary 05C10, 05C70. Keywords: Barnette’s conjecture, Hamiltonian cycle, Eulerian triangulation, perfect matching, spanning tree. Add 05C85 only if an algorithm is a substantive result.

## Historical paragraph

Tait’s conjecture that every cubic polyhedral graph is Hamiltonian was disproved by Tutte in 1946; Tutte’s later conjecture for cubic bipartite 3-connected graphs was disproved by Horton’s 96-vertex graph. Barnette’s planar bipartite version is recorded as Conjecture 5, p. 343, in Tutte’s edited *Recent Progress in Combinatorics* (1969), the proceedings of the 1968 Waterloo conference; [Alt et al.’s reference 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3783) supports this citation, but the original page has not been verified here, and recording in 1969 should not be confused with the date of first formulation. Goodey proved the quadrilateral–hexagonal case in 1975; Holton, Manvel and McKay verified the conjecture through 64 vertices in 1985, and [Brinkmann, Goedgebeur and McKay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.00943) through 90 in their 2022 publication. Kelmans established equivalent strengthened formulations, including prescribed edge behaviour; distinguish the 1986 Russian work from its 1994 translation. Akiyama, Nishizeki and Saito proved NP-completeness for the 2-connected cubic bipartite planar class in 1980; its unique smallest non-Hamiltonian graph has 26 vertices, as proved by [Asano, Saito, Exoo and Harary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0012365X82901625). Further structural approaches include Feder–Subi and Bagheri Gh. et al.; [Kardoš](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.2440) proved the related faces-at-most-six theorem without bipartiteness. The supplied OpenAI manuscript, dated 24 September 2026, presents the paired-state proof; Li and the unsigned constructive manuscript give subsequent accounts.
