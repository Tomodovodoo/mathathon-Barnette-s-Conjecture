                               SYMMETRIC FLOATING BODIES

       Abstract. We construct nonspherical, centrally symmetric, strictly convex bodies of revolu-
       tion in four dimensions that have real-analytic boundaries and float in equilibrium in every
       orientation at a homogeneous relative density different from one half. Their densities approach
       an explicitly specified value near 0.218294 as the bodies approach the unit ball. The proof com-
       bines an all-degree arithmetic estimate for a spherical cap operator with an exact differential
       reduction and an analytic iteration on a set of admissible chord angles. The scalar volume and
       density conditions are included in the inverse, and estimates for actual operator increments
       preserve a positive width of analyticity throughout the iteration.




                            1. Statement and analytic conventions
    A body of uniform relative density ρ ∈ (0, 1) floats in equilibrium in a direction n if a
hyperplane normal to n cuts off the volume ρ vol K and the line joining the centroids of K
and the submerged part is parallel to n. This is the customary geometric formulation of Ulam’s
floating-body problem; see [5]. Ryabogin constructed nonspherical bodies at density one half [4],
whereas central symmetry at that density entails rigidity [2]. Formal perturbation constructions
for other densities were developed by Wegner [6]. Here we construct exact analytic bodies with
central symmetry at densities separated from one half.
