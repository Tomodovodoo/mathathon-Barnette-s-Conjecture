# Integration wording

This text separates independently replayed computations, author-reported runs, and general proofs. It uses q for the number of Q_s cycles, to avoid collision with the glossary's k. Here V is the number of vertices of the dual triangulation and N is the number in the primal cubic graph.

## C3 and failed rules

CONJECTURE C3. From every pair in a rooted simple Eulerian sphere triangulation, a good pair can be reached by exchanges reversing one D-cycle or one Q_s-cycle, without ever increasing q.

COMPUTED. An independent implementation reproduced C3 on all 24,744 ordered pairs of the complete plantri census through V=16, including all facial roots. It reproduced 24,090 good pairs and exactly two nongood strict local minima, both at V=16. The original descent report records a larger census of 49,325,864 pairs through V=22 and 9,970,784 pairs of 230 random triangulations with V=26 to 80. Those larger figures are author-reported ranges, not same-size independent replays by this referee. No general proof of C3 is supplied.

REFUTED. Strict decrease of q at every exchange fails first at V=16, independently confirmed by exhaustive comparison at smaller orders. A saved V=21 witness requires three exchanges before q falls, refuting radius-two lookahead. Its minimality is reported by the original V<=22 census. A saved V=60 witness requires four exchanges, refuting radius-three lookahead. The report also observes a radius-four pair at V=40, but its saved V=40 RHO certificate has radius three and the radius-four pair still needs a complete independent certificate. The smallest independently replayed radius-four witness in this review is therefore V=60. No claim that every fixed lookahead fails is justified; radius four remains open.

REFUTED. Paths constrained never to increase c, the number of cover cycles, fail at V=16. Named lexicographic height rules fail at V=16 for equal height signs and V=18 for opposite signs. Restricting Q plateau moves to one height direction fails at V=18; a saved V=40 witness refutes the analogous D-height rule, even with two-move macros in another saved example. The size/area rules q with vq or wq are reported to fail at V=40, but their selected certificate lines are truncated. Do not replace this list by 'every potential involving c fails' or 'every bounded variant is false.' The claim '27 of 28 tested potentials fail' concerns a fixed list and should carry the individual certificate coverage in the referee report.

PROVED-HERE, independently refereed, human check pending. A D-swap destroys precisely the old Q-cycles meeting its D-cycle; it can create new cycles there. Three normalized integer heights w_r,w_s,w_u have constant sum. A D-swap transfers the enclosed white-face count between w_r and w_s; a Q-reversal transfers it between w_r and w_u. White-face gluing of pair-admitting pieces gives a Cartesian product of exchange graphs, with additive q and c. It preserves C3 and takes the maximum of the factor escape radii. Thus white-face chains of a fixed successful instance cannot supply a C3 counterexample.

## Corrected complexity paragraph

PROVED-HERE, conditional on the supplied papers' existence and marked-walk theorems. The Hamiltonian-cycle search problem, totalized by allowing rejection of graphs outside the stated class, belongs to TFNP. The supplied constructive paper gives deterministic O(poly(N) 3^(N/4)) time and polynomial space by matching enumeration, and a marked walk with 2^O(N log N) time and polynomial space. These arguments give no polynomial-time construction or hardness result. For a supplied pair, the marked involutions yield a PPADS reduction: join unsuccessful phases, whose cover counts decrease by at least two, using a polynomial-size history so that every sink represents a good pair. A restart may itself already be good and must be retained as a useful sink. PPADS is contained in PPP. Extending the claim to the full graph uses polynomial-time decomposition, flow, reconstruction, and the closure of PPADS under polynomial-time Turing reductions established by Buss and Johnson. This reduction does not establish PPAD, PPA, or PLS membership. C3 alone does not supply a PLS formulation because it allows equal-q plateaus. For an efficiently computable potential with polynomially many values and fixed-radius exchange neighborhoods, a guarantee that every local optimum is good would already give a polynomial-time algorithm by repeated strict improvement. The named tested potentials fail that guarantee.

Source qualification. The class definitions and inclusions were checked against Li, Pires and Robere, ITCS 2024, section 1. Buss and Johnson, Propositional proofs and reductions between NP search problems, Annals of Pure and Applied Logic 163, 2012, pages 1163-1182, state the closure result in the abstract; the full publisher text was inaccessible in this review. If that citation is to carry a formal theorem in the manuscript, retrieve and check its theorem statement. Do not use an unspecified 'NP-hardness' assertion for a search problem. The standard obstruction is FNP-hardness under polynomial search reductions implying NP=coNP.

## State of contraction induction

PROVED-HERE, independently refereed, human check pending. For connected induced complement U of a nonroot set S, the Hall deficiency is sum_F(d_F+w_F-6)/3 over nonactual face regions, counting boundary edge sides with multiplicity. Degree-four contraction changes the deficiency by the stated two indicator terms when the merged vertex is absent, and preserves it after expanding that vertex to the two split copies and x when it is present. Under the no-separating-triangle hypothesis, the exact bad-merge witnesses are chordless four-cycles causing parallel edges and chordless pentagons whose root-free side has four black and one white boundary incidence, causing pair loss in a simple contraction. The four-cycle-only description is false.

PROVED-HERE. If V>6 and T has neither separating triangles nor chordless pentagons, every nonroot degree-four vertex has at least one simple pair-preserving contraction. This does not settle the general selection problem.

PROVED-HERE. A contracted good state has a unique lift. If the lift fails, the three-face rotation succeeds except for a precisely defined exception: in the split forest, a,p are in one component, and after deleting qc the component of q still contains b. Rotation bijects the exception sets E_M and E_N. Writing e for their common size gives

    #good(T) >= #good(T/M)+#good(T/N)-2e.

Successful direct lifts and successful rotations form four disjoint image sets. If the other contraction has no pair, then e=0 and lift-or-rotate supplies the one-sided induction step. Therefore a finite reduction certificate using octahedron leaves, white-face splitting, and such one-sided contractions gives a cancellation-free proof for its input. Not every input is known to possess that certificate.

CONJECTURE G, the first missing lemma. Every rooted simple Eulerian sphere triangulation with V>6 and no separating triangle has a nonroot degree-four vertex with at least one simple contraction admitting a pair. A test must examine both contraction directions at all such vertices. The short-cycle classification supplies obstructions but no global selection argument avoiding all of them.

CONJECTURE X, the second missing lemma. At a degree-four vertex where both contractions have good states, at least one contraction has a good state whose unique lift or prescribed three-face rotation is good in T. Equivalently, not both good-state sets consist entirely of exceptions. This is a sufficient condition for the induction step, not an equivalence deduced from existence of a good state in T. The stronger bounds 2e<=#good(T/M) and 2e<=#good(T/N) would imply X and both one-sided counting inequalities.

COMPUTED. Independent enumeration through V=18 confirms the rotation bijection and counting inequality, all 7,940 exceptions, corner avoidance at every bounded black face whenever good states exist, and the half-exception bound at every contraction. Neither finite assertion is promoted to a general lemma. The V=11,13,14 saved examples refute the respective inward-selection strategies for G. The V=18 example has an exceptional good state in a one-cycle cover whose only other orientation is nongood, so a universal repair for X must sometimes change the cover. These are failures of proposed proof strategies, not counterexamples to G, X, or the supplied Barnette existence theorem.
