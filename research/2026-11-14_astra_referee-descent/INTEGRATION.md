# Integration wording

This text separates independently replayed computations, author-reported runs, and general proofs. It uses q for the number of Q_s cycles, to avoid collision with the glossary's k. Here V is the number of vertices of the dual triangulation and N is the number in the primal cubic graph.

For potential names, c counts cover cycles, vq counts vertices on Q_s cycles, and wq and wd sum the enclosed white-face counts over Q_s cycles and D-cycles respectively. The heights om=w_s and psi=w_u use the descent report's rotation convention; reflection reverses both signs. Tuples are compared lexicographically. A weak-descent trap permits equal-cost moves; a strict-improvement obstruction merely has no lower-cost point in the prescribed exchange ball.

## C3 and failed rules

CONJECTURE C3. From every pair in a rooted simple Eulerian sphere triangulation, a good pair can be reached by exchanges reversing one D-cycle or one Q_s-cycle, without ever increasing q.

COMPUTED. Independent implementations reproduced C3 on all 3,373,400 ordered pairs of the complete plantri census through V=20, including all facial roots, and all 335,704 pairs at all roots of the recorded 40-graph V=40 batch. The V<=20 census has 3,168,818 good pairs and 3,289 nongood local optima for strict descent. The first two such minima occur at V=16. The original descent report records a larger census of 49,325,864 pairs through V=22 and 9,970,784 pairs of 230 random triangulations with V=26 to 80. Those larger figures are author-reported ranges, not same-size independent replays by this referee. An additional independently generated sample contains 15 rooted inputs at V=26,30,34 and 70 pairs, all passing. The listed lattice gluings, prism sums, nanotubes and separator-free one-cover examples also pass in the explicit ranges in code/families.json and code/adversarial_extra.json. No general proof of C3 is supplied.

REFUTED. Strict decrease of q at every exchange fails first at V=16, independently confirmed by exhaustive comparison at smaller orders. A saved V=21 witness requires three exchanges before q falls, refuting radius-two lookahead. The independent V<=20 census has no pair with escape radius above two, so V=21 is confirmed to be the least order. A recovered V=40 witness requires four exchanges, refuting radius-three lookahead. Its complete independent certificate is graph 3, root {10,16,11}, in code/recovered40_0_40.json. The saved V=60 example independently gives the same radius. V=40 is not claimed to be the least order. No claim that every fixed lookahead fails is justified; radius four remains open.

REFUTED. Paths constrained never to increase c, the number of cover cycles, fail at V=16. Named lexicographic height rules fail at V=16 for equal height signs and V=18 for opposite signs. Restricting Q plateau moves to one height direction fails at V=18; a saved V=40 witness refutes the analogous D-height rule, even with two-move macros in another saved example. The size/area rules vq, wq, (q,vq), (q,wq) fail at V=40; this review recovered complete certificates from the regenerated input, replacing the truncated selected lines. Do not replace this list by 'every potential involving c fails' or 'every bounded variant is false.' The claim '27 of 28 tested potentials fail' concerns a fixed list and should carry the individual certificate coverage in the referee report.

COMPUTED. All eight signed three-entry height potentials also have independently checked radius-two strict-improvement obstructions at V=40. The four size/area rules have complete radius-two weak-trap certificates at V=50. These larger orders are witness sizes, not proved minima. A second independently generated sample, restricted to separator-free graphs, checks 1,160 pairs on 160 roots of 20 graphs at V=26,30,34,40; all pass C3. Both independent insertion samples are biased.

PROVED-HERE, independently refereed, human check pending. A D-swap destroys precisely the old Q-cycles meeting its D-cycle; it can create new cycles there. Three normalized integer heights w_r,w_s,w_u have constant sum. A D-swap transfers the enclosed white-face count between w_r and w_s; a Q-reversal transfers it between w_r and w_u. White-face gluing of pair-admitting pieces gives a Cartesian product of exchange graphs, with additive q and c. It preserves C3 and takes the maximum of the factor escape radii. Thus white-face chains of a fixed successful instance cannot supply a C3 counterexample.

## Corrected complexity paragraph

PROVED-HERE, conditional on the supplied papers' existence and marked-walk theorems. The Hamiltonian-cycle search problem, totalized by allowing rejection of graphs outside the stated class, belongs to TFNP. The supplied constructive paper gives deterministic O(poly(N) 3^(N/4)) time and polynomial space by matching enumeration, and a marked walk with 2^O(N log N) time and polynomial space. These arguments give no polynomial-time construction or hardness result. For a supplied pair, the marked involutions yield a PPADS reduction: join unsuccessful phases, whose cover counts decrease by at least two, using a polynomial-size history so that every sink represents a good pair. At an unsuccessful alpha-fixed endpoint all cover cycles are already counterclockwise, so restarting changes marks but preserves the nongood pair and yields a valid new source. PPADS is contained in PPP. Extending the claim to the full graph uses polynomial-time decomposition, flow, reconstruction, and the closure of PPADS under polynomial-time Turing reductions established by Buss and Johnson. This reduction does not establish PPAD, PPA, or PLS membership. C3 alone does not supply a PLS formulation because it allows equal-q plateaus. For an efficiently computable potential with polynomially many values and fixed-radius exchange neighborhoods, a guarantee that every local optimum is good would already give a polynomial-time algorithm by repeated strict improvement. The named tested potentials fail that guarantee.

Source qualification. The class definitions and inclusions were checked against Li, Pires and Robere, ITCS 2024, section 1. The closure result is Corollary 6.2 in the July 6, 2011 author version of Buss and Johnson, Propositional proofs and reductions between NP search problems, published in Annals of Pure and Applied Logic 163, 2012, pages 1163-1182. Its statement was checked in indexed primary PDF text; direct full-PDF access failed. See [the author manuscript](https://www.math.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/NPSearch/NPSearch.pdf). Do not use an unspecified 'NP-hardness' assertion for a search problem. The standard obstruction is FNP-hardness under polynomial search reductions implying NP=coNP.

## State of contraction induction

PROVED-HERE, independently refereed, human check pending. Define f_T(S)=sum over bounded black faces t of min(2,|t intersect S|)-2|S|. If the complement U=V(T) minus S induces a connected graph, then f_T(S)=sum_F(d_F+w_F-6)/3 over its nonactual face regions, counting boundary edge sides with multiplicity; w_F counts white incidences on the region side.

At a nonroot degree-four vertex x, write the cyclic neighbors a,p,b,q with black faces xap and xbq. Contraction M merges p,q to v, while N merges a,b. For a nonroot set S of T/M, if v is absent then f_(T/M)(S)=f_T(S)-[a in S]-[b in S]. If v is present then f_(T/M)(S)=f_T((S minus {v}) union {p,q,x}). Under the no-separating-triangle hypothesis, the exact bad-merge witnesses for M are chordless four-cycles x,p,y,q causing parallel edges, or chordless pentagons x,p,y,z,q whose root-free side has four black and one white boundary incidence, causing pair loss when the contraction is simple. The four-cycle-only description is false.

PROVED-HERE. If V>6 and T has neither separating triangles nor chordless pentagons, every nonroot degree-four vertex has at least one simple pair-preserving contraction. This does not settle the general selection problem.

PROVED-HERE. A contracted good state has a unique lift. If the lift fails, the three-face rotation succeeds except for a precisely defined exception: in the split forest, a,p are in one component, and after deleting qc the component of q still contains b. Rotation bijects the exception sets E_M and E_N. Writing e for their common size gives

    #good(T) >= #good(T/M)+#good(T/N)-2e.

Successful direct lifts and successful rotations form four disjoint image sets. If the other contraction has no pair, then e=0 and lift-or-rotate supplies the one-sided induction step. Therefore a finite reduction certificate using octahedron leaves, white-face splitting, and such one-sided contractions gives a cancellation-free proof for its input. Not every input is known to possess that certificate.

CONJECTURE G, the first missing lemma. Every rooted simple Eulerian sphere triangulation with V>6 and no separating triangle has a nonroot degree-four vertex with at least one simple contraction admitting a pair. A test must examine both contraction directions at all such vertices. The short-cycle classification supplies obstructions but no global selection argument avoiding all of them.

CONJECTURE X, the second missing lemma. For a rooted simple Eulerian sphere triangulation T with no separating triangle and a nonroot degree-four vertex x where both contractions have good states, at least one contraction has a good state whose unique lift or prescribed three-face rotation is good in T. Equivalently, not both good-state sets consist entirely of exceptions. This is a sufficient condition for the induction step, not an equivalence deduced from existence of a good state in T. The stronger bounds 2e<=#good(T/M) and 2e<=#good(T/N) would imply X and both one-sided counting inequalities.

COMPUTED. Independent enumeration through V=18 confirms the rotation bijection and counting inequality, all 7,940 exceptions, corner avoidance at every bounded black face whenever good states exist, and the half-exception bound at every contraction. Neither finite assertion is promoted to a general lemma. The V=11,13,14 saved examples refute the respective inward-selection strategies for G. The V=18 example has an exceptional good state in a one-cycle cover whose only other orientation is nongood, so a universal repair for X must sometimes change the cover. These are failures of proposed proof strategies, not counterexamples to G, X, or the supplied Barnette existence theorem.

COMPUTED. The complete exceptional-cover replay finds no such fixed-cover obstruction below V=18 and 220 rooted contraction-cover records at V=18. The stronger per-cover half bound already fails at V=17. The outer-state correspondence used in the rotation proof must be restricted to states with acyclic augmented outer graph; its unrestricted wording has a V=8 counterexample. The required good-state domain satisfies this restriction, so the counting theorem is unaffected.
