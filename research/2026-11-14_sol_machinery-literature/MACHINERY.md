# Machinery behind the paired-state proof

Notation follows `research/BRIEF.md`. O is OpenAI, L is Li, C is the constructive paper. Bracketed names are bibliography keys. `LIT` includes an explicitly identified preprint claim, without certifying its proof or peer review. `PROVED-HERE` arguments appear here or in `REFORMULATIONS.md`. `HUNCH` also marks unverified identifications. Sources were retrieved on 2026-11-14; hashes are in `downloads.json`. The unslop skill was read and applied.

## What the three papers use

[LIT, supplied preprints] O's dependency chain is Euler counting and integral max flow, Proposition 3.1; the disk identity, Lemma 4.1; fixed-second-state cancellation, Lemma 5.2; positive face circulations, Lemma 5.3; least-degree nonvanishing, Section 5.3; complementary plane trees, Lemma 2.1 and Section 6.1; acyclicity and inducedness, Section 6.2; and separator gluing, Section 7. Trinity, Temperley and double dimers explain the objects, but O proves its needed properties. Its final Pfaffian corollary imports an equivalence from Gorsky, Steiner and Wiederrecht rather than extending the planar construction directly.

[LIT, supplied preprints] L obtains the initial 2-factor through the bipartite factor criterion called Ore's theorem in Theorem 10 and the deficit argument in Lemma 9. Its Claim 25 is the disk Euler count in primal incidence notation. Lemmas 16 and 17 control the sign under two reversals. Definitions 22 and 23 give involutions, with fixed points and sign properties in Lemmas 24, 34 and 35. Appendix B handles minimum cycle count greater than one, a case excluded temporarily in the main text. Lemma 8 and Corollary 27 give tree/cotree duality. Lemma 30 traces a white tree to recover the Hamiltonian cycle. Appendix C glues across 3-edge cuts.

[LIT, supplied preprints] C adds the disjoint-mate count, Proposition 2.1; the exact black inner-boundary separating-triangle obstruction, Section 3; a graph of exchanges on pairs, Section 4; componentwise nonvanishing of an integer polynomial, Theorem 5.2; an alternating marked walk, Theorem 5.3; determinant counting and enumeration, Section 6; and recovery and recursion, Section 7. Its time estimate uses Hadamard and n=N/2-1. This review records the supplied claims and identifies their machinery; it is not a full independent proof audit.

## Trinity and the magic number

[LIT] Tutte's *Duality and trinity* is the 1975 chapter, pages 1459-1472 in volume III of *Infinite and Finite Sets*. I checked its identity against Berman's publisher references and Hine-Kalman's references, but did not obtain the original. Berman's article is *A proof of Tutte's trinity theorem and a new determinant formula*, SIAM Journal on Algebraic Discrete Methods 1, 1980, 64-69, DOI 10.1137/0601009. The publisher misspells Tutte as “Tuite.” Its abstract is open; its proof was not obtained. The downloaded [Kalman2013, Sections 9-10] gives a checked modern account, including Theorems 9.9 and 9.10.

[LIT, HineKalman2018, Theorems 4.2-4.3, Lemma 4.4] A planar trinity has three properly colored vertex classes and compatible black/white triangles. Remove one white triangle and its vertices. The remaining triangle-vertex matchings correspond bijectively to spanning arborescences in each of three directed dual graphs. Their common count is the magic number. States form a distributive lattice under clock moves. These are single-state moves at empty opposite-colored triangles, with connected sums treated separately. Hine-Kalman's arrows end at the matched vertices; reverse arrows when adopting an in-arborescence convention.

[PROVED-HERE] O's face-colored sphere triangulation has the required vertex coloring. Give one triangle three distinct labels and propagate across adjacent triangles. Around any vertex the two other labels alternate. Its degree is even because black and white faces alternate, so propagation returns consistently around it. These local dual loops generate all monodromy on the sphere, hence propagation is globally consistent. Swapping black and white now identifies O's states exactly with planar Tutte states. On a torus this propagation can have monodromy.

[LIT, Kalman2013, Theorem 9.10; C, Lemma 6.1] If K_B has rows A, columns X, and entries one for incidence, all nonzero determinant terms have the same sign. Thus the number of O-states is |det K_B| and equals the magic number. The counted arborescences live in the three trinity digraphs, not arbitrary H or H*. C's count is an instance of Berman's theorem with an elementary proof and an algorithmic Hadamard bound.

[PROVED-HERE, also C Proposition 2.1] A mate r of s orients each opposite edge from r(t) to the third corner. Nonroots have outdegree one, roots zero. A component with v vertices and a roots has v-a edges. Connectedness forces a<=1, hence either a tree with one root or a rootless unicycle. Conversely such components admit the required orientation, uniquely on rooted trees and in two directions on each unicycle. Reading tails gives r. Therefore a state has no mate unless every component has that form; otherwise it has 2^k mates, k the rootless unicycle count. A tree matching has exactly one mate.

[HUNCH] I found no result in the checked trinity sources that solves disjoint-pair packing with the additional forest condition. Two states give two triples of coupled arborescences, subject to inequality at every triangle. Equality of counts does not supply those inequalities. The tree trinity theorem alone does not finish this proof.

## Hypertrees and their polynomials

[LIT, Kalman2013, Definition 3.1, Theorem 3.4] A hypertree on hyperedges E is a nonnegative integer vector f realized by an incidence spanning tree of degree f(e)+1 at each e. Its inequalities are sum f=|V|-1 and, for nonempty E', sum over E' f <= |union E'|-c(E'), with c the component count of the incidence subgraph on E' and its neighbors. These lattice points form an integral polymatroid base polytope. The interior polynomial enumerates internal inactivity, independently of the auxiliary order. A hypertree records degrees, not its possibly many realizing trees.

[PROVED-HERE] For incidence graph H the desired center hypertree is f(t)=1 on A and f(t0)=2. Simultaneously the realizing tree must have degrees d_H(x)-1 on X and d_H(v) on R. Two separately feasible degree vectors need a COMMON realizing tree. Merely testing the center hypertree drops half the condition. The exact common-realization coefficient is in `REFORMULATIONS.md`.

[LIT, KalmanPostnikov2017] For a connected bipartite graph its interior polynomial is equivalent to the Ehrhart polynomial of its root polytope, and to the h-vector of any triangulation. The two hypergraphs obtained by exchanging vertex classes have equal interior polynomials. This gives degree-data symmetry, not simultaneous realization of a prescribed pair of vectors. O, L and C do not invoke it.

[LIT, KalmanMurakami2017, Theorem 1.1 and abstract] Trees complementary to directed dual arborescences give a root-polytope triangulation of a plane bipartite graph. The paper relates its h-polynomial, a parking-function enumerator and the top HOMFLY part of a special alternating link, with normalizations. This is an exact neighboring theory for trinity graphs. It does not assert that deleting O's state from H leaves a tree.

[LIT, JKR2012, Theorem 1.1] The supporting Spin^c structures of sutured Floer homology of a special alternating Seifert-surface complement are affinely identified with a hypertree set of an associated hypergraph. This is a genuine homological model of degree-vector data. None of the three Barnette papers uses it. [HUNCH] No Floer class forcing simultaneous two-sided degrees or disjoint mates was identified here.

[LIT, KalmanTothmeresz2020, Theorem 4.5 and abstract] “Kalman-Toth” appears to mean Kálmán and Lilla Tóthmérész. Their ribbon-graph Bernardi process selects tree representatives of hypertrees and yields an activity formula for the interior polynomial using root-polytope dissections and shellings. [HUNCH] The useful question here is whether a chosen representative preserves the OTHER prescribed degree vector. That is an extra condition, absent from the general theorem.

[LIT, BKP2022] Bernardi, Kálmán and Postnikov extend the two-variable Tutte polynomial to polymatroids and define a universal polynomial with translation, permutation and duality symmetries. [HUNCH] No specialization yielding O's Z has been found. Such a map must retain the embedding, circulation weights and ordered disjoint-pair condition. The word “universal” does not supply it.

## Clock lattices and heights

[LIT, Kauffman1983, checked through HineKalman2018 Section 2.2] Kauffman's states select incident regions at crossings, with two adjacent regions excluded. Clock transpositions connect the states and give unique extrema. Gilmer-Litherland's *The duality conjecture in formal knot theory*, Osaka Journal of Mathematics 23, 1986, 229-247, supplies the lattice refinement discussed by Hine-Kalman. The original book and original Gilmer-Litherland proof were not read; their theorem numbering and exact original hypotheses are unverified. Hine-Kalman's proved generalization is the appropriate direct source here.

[LIT, Propp2002] Orientations with fixed circulations, after fixing the distinguished accessibility class, admit distributive lattice orders through local reversals. Plane duality yields the prescribed-indegree form. Degree-constrained plane bipartite factors, including perfect matchings, have related lattices. Relative to a reference matching, the difference is divergence-free and becomes a dual gradient of an integer height function, normalized on the exterior. Appropriate height functions admit meet and join. O, L and C do not use the lattice theorem.

[PROVED-HERE] A pair lattice does not follow formally. The condition r(t)!=s(t) is an exclusion between two matchings, and coordinatewise meets or joins require a separate proof that exclusion survives. C's exchanges act on ordered pairs: some change r alone, others reverse both matchings around a union cycle. This is not Hine-Kalman's graph on individual states. [HUNCH] Differences of state heights may still supply a potential, but its behavior must be checked for both move types.

## Trees, matchings and orientations

[LIT, KPW2000, Theorem 1] Temperley's bijection extends to a weight-preserving correspondence between rooted directed trees of a plane graph and matchings of an associated overlay graph. Delete a root vertex and an incident root face; the matching records primal and complementary dual rooted trees through edge centers. The root-face incidence is essential in the basic form. One cannot apply it to arbitrary B without constructing that overlay. O Section 6 and C Section 7 supply their own triangular constructions and cite a relation, not an input. L's black trace is another explicit construction.

[LIT, BurtonPemantle1993] In a uniform spanning tree, the probability that a prescribed edge set occurs is a determinant of its transfer-current kernel minor, with consistent orientation conventions. This gives exact tree marginals and tree/tiling connections. None of O, L, C invokes it. [HUNCH] It might estimate the desired joint degree event. Positivity of separate vertex marginals does not establish positive probability for their intersection.

[LIT, Bernardi2008] A ribbon structure and root half-edge give bijections between subgraphs and orientations. Specializations include trees versus root-connected outdegree sequences, and trees versus recurrent sandpile configurations. This does not select arbitrary prescribed individual orientations while retaining every local constraint. O and L orient and trace trees directly. [HUNCH] A sandpile route needs the precise images of the matching and degree conditions first.

[PROVED-HERE] Complementary plane-tree duality is the exact classical theorem all three use. Contract a primal spanning tree in a connected sphere graph. Complementary dual edges remain connected and number |F|-1 by Euler, hence form a dual tree. Extending a forest to a tree proves connectivity for the complementary dual of any forest. Bridges and parallel edges cause no problem. On genus g the complement has 2g extra edges. O Lemma 2.1, L Corollary 27 and C Section 7.1 are instances of this theorem.

## Schnyder woods and map encodings

[LIT, FPS2009] Schnyder woods are colored directed tree triples with local cyclic-order rules on triangulations. Fusy, Poulalhon and Schaeffer's orientation bijection specializes to the Bernardi-Bonichon encoding by noncrossing Dyck paths. Their *Dissections, orientations, and trees* gives another map/tree encoding. Bonichon's original bijection was checked through these authors, not separately read.

[HUNCH] Schnyder trees must not be identified with trinity arborescences merely because there are three. Their graphs, roots and arrow rules differ. Applying ordinary Schnyder machinery to an Eulerian triangulation does not yet encode s or its mate. None of the three Barnette papers uses Schnyder woods. The missing map must retain one chosen corner per black face and acyclicity of its opposite edges.

[LIT, BMS2002] Bousquet-Mélou and Schaeffer encode degree-constrained bipartite maps by decorated trees. The downloaded source is *The degree distribution in bipartite planar maps: applications to the Ising model*, arXiv math/0211070, revised October 2026. Its cubic dual specialization concerns Eulerian triangulations. Schaeffer's 1997 *Bijective census and random generation of Eulerian planar maps with prescribed vertex degrees* is a different paper, located but not fully read. Fusy's author page confirms related canonical-orientation encodings. [HUNCH] These bijections are useful for generating test maps, but no checked theorem selects the required tree matching on every map. Do not invent a joint FPS theorem specifically about the Barnette state condition.

[LIT, BMSConstellations1999, Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.2 and Sections 3-4] A further original source was obtained from the FPSAC 1999 archive. An m-constellation has black faces of degree m and white faces of degree a multiple of m. Its labeled version corresponds to transitive permutation factorizations, and the authors encode it with bicolored trees. This conference version omits the full bijectivity proof and points to its longer source. [PROVED-HERE] O's T is the special 3-constellation in which every white face also has degree three. Thus the constellation tree encoding genuinely contains these maps. It encodes the underlying map, not the pair of face-vertex transversals with the forest condition. [HUNCH] The exact specialization of the decoder to that extra condition remains to be worked out.

[LIT, Bonichon2002, conference record checked] The exact original title is *A bijection between realizers of maximal plane graphs and pairs of non-crossing Dyck paths*, FPSAC 2002, pages 123-132. The public PDF URL was found, but its host failed TLS certificate validation during download. Its theorem is used here through FPS2009, not an unread original proof.

## Pfaffians and double dimers

[LIT, Kenyon2011, Sections 3.1-3.3] A planar dimer partition function is a Kasteleyn Pfaffian, or a signed bipartite determinant, up to a global phase. In a Pfaffian orientation the COMPLETE terms have the same sign, including edge signs. Two dimer partition functions multiply. Regrouping pairs gives doubled edges and alternating loops; each loop receives the sum of its two alternating weights. For an SL2 connection the loop weight is its monodromy trace and the partition function is a quaternion determinant, reducible to a block determinant for a bipartite graph. This is the checked modern source for the historical Kasteleyn/Percus machinery. Percus's 1969 original was not accessible.

[PROVED-HERE] B has a stronger sign property than generic planar dimers. Given two states, their differing faces give directed relative-permutation cycles. The disk identity makes each cycle's delta sum +/-3. Its length is odd because each delta is odd. Every relative-permutation cycle is therefore an even permutation, so all state permutations have the same sign. This proves C Lemma 6.1 from the disk identity and explains why all-positive K_B already counts states.

[PROVED-HERE] Z has a local turn interaction q^{delta(u,v)} exp(x a(t,v)), q=exp(pi i/6). It is not immediately a product of scalar dimer sums. Around the two directed 3-cycles on a face, the ratio of the products of q-weights is q^6=-1. A factorization alpha(t,u) beta(t,v) would give ratio one. This disproves scalar separation when all three corners are permitted. It does not disprove a gadget or larger determinant. An exact determinant-COEFFICIENT formula is proved in `REFORMULATIONS.md`.

[PROVED-HERE] The loop factor -i exp(xL+)+i exp(xL-) equals -2i exp(xm) sinh(x Delta/2), where m=(L++L-)/2 and Delta=L+-L-. For Delta>0 it has a simple zero at zero; a group has order c(D). Groups minimizing c(D) have a common leading phase. This is O's finite nonvanishing calculation. For real x the exponential weight is not unitary. Imaginary x makes that part unitary but changes the positivity argument. A flat U(1) connection on the unpunctured sphere has trivial contractible holonomy, so it cannot realize positive area circulations. Curvature, punctures or a turn gadget would be required. No complete trace identification was proved here.

## Cancellation and polynomial methods

[LIT, GarsiaMilne1981, PNAS announcement] The involution principle alternates two cancellations on a finite signed set. The graph of nonfixed moves has degree at most two; a walk starting at an unmatched endpoint ends at another endpoint. Finiteness does not give a polynomial bound. L uses this pattern for existence. C's ordered token marks and cycle-count descent implement a constructor. Those marks and the descent are specific work, not a conclusion of the general principle.

[PROVED-HERE] A sign-reversing involution cancels its two-element orbits, so the signed total equals its fixed-point total. If one involution has a nonempty fixed set of one sign, the total is nonzero. A second sign-reversing involution must have a fixed point. This is L's logical template. Proving that its maps preserve marks and signs is the substantive part.

[LIT, Lindstrom1973; GesselViennot1985, Kuperberg2002, Theorem 6, source in SOURCES.md] In an acyclic weighted network, the determinant of the source-sink path matrix is the signed sum of vertex-disjoint path families, with endpoint-permutation sign. Intersecting families cancel by swapping tails at a consistently selected intersection. Planar endpoint order can force only the identity permutation. O, L and C do not construct such a network. [HUNCH] Cutting paired cycles open may produce one, but seams, endpoint permutations and acyclicity need proofs.

[LIT, Chaiken1982, publisher abstract and matrix-tree formulation] A Laplacian principal cofactor counts trees. Deleting equal-size row and column sets I,J gives a signed forest sum with one I-vertex and one J-vertex in each component and the corresponding pairing sign. This controls specified root connections. It does not enforce that forest edges arise from a face-vertex perfect matching. O, L and C use elementary counts rather than all minors. The degree-coefficient reformulation below is an instance of weighted matrix-tree expansion.

[LIT, Alon1999, Theorem 1.2] If total degree is sum t_i and the coefficient of product x_i^{t_i} is nonzero, then a polynomial is nonzero at some point of any product of sets S_i with |S_i|>t_i. Alon-Tarsi instead detects a nonzero difference between even and odd Eulerian subdigraph counts of an orientation and obtains a list-coloring bound. The original Alon-Tarsi scan was downloaded and Theorem 1.1 checked; its full proof was not audited. O has the same signed-nonvanishing logic. It is not already a Nullstellensatz application, since its needed nonzero coefficient remains to be proved and its desired object is a state.

## Matroids and Eulerian systems

[LIT, Rado1942 original record; Schrijver Theorem 5.26] Rado's independent-transversal criterion is r_M(union of A_i for i in I)>=|I| for all I. Edmonds's two-matroid theorem is max |J|=min_U r_1(U)+r_2(E minus U). Schrijver gives the checked accessible intersection statement. Rado's original proof was not obtained.

[PROVED-HERE] On incidences the selection has THREE matroid constraints: one per face, one per nonroot, and graphic independence of opposite edges. The first two are partition matroids. Identifying the three roots before testing the third graphic matroid enforces both acyclicity and separation of roots. Retain resulting loops and parallel edges. A common independent set of size n is exactly a tree matching. This is not automatically an instance of two-matroid intersection.

[REFUTED] “Matchings are one transversal matroid, so intersect with the graphic matroid” fails on that ground set. In K_2,2 let I={a1x1}, J={a1x2,a2x1}. Both are matchings, |I|<|J|, but no edge of J augments I. Transversal matroids have their ground set on ONE vertex class. Passing to that ground set forgets the actual chosen corners needed to define Q_s. A different reduction remains possible, but this one fails.

[LIT, Lovasz1980 publisher abstract; LovaszRecski1982] Matroid parity asks for an independent union of specified disjoint pairs. Linear representability permits algebraic algorithms unavailable for arbitrary oracle matroids. [HUNCH] Retaining two spokes at a center resembles parity, but the three candidate pairs overlap, and X-degrees couple centers. A reduction needs copies, exclusion constraints and a representation. It is not supplied by the analogy.

[LIT, Bouchet1989, publisher record only] Bouchet associates delta-matroids to cellular maps. [HUNCH, original proof inaccessible] The usual ribbon-graph interpretation uses spanning quasi-trees, and Eulerian transition systems have related symmetric exchanges. I have not checked those precise original statements. A genus-zero specialization should be compared to ordinary trees before using it. The matching restriction remains additional data.

[HUNCH] Rota's basis conjecture is an analogy about arranging compatible bases. This problem does not supply an array of n bases of one rank-n matroid, and no implication was found. Likewise generic matroid exchange does not prove C's theorem that every pair-exchange component contains a tree pair. That is precisely its extra conclusion.

## Topology, categories and invariants

[LIT, Stein1971, Theorem 2.2] Stein's B-set theorem identifies a Hamiltonian boundary with a two-class partition of regions excluding the relevant region cycles. Its proof explicitly uses Alexander duality. This is a genuine homological ancestor of the induced-tree formulation. O uses its elementary dual version, also citing Alt et al.; L traces a tree boundary; C uses the same recovery.

[PROVED-HERE] A graph has vanishing H_1 over a field exactly when it is a forest. A nonempty graph is a tree exactly when reduced H_0 and H_1 vanish. This gives an exact homological restatement of two induced trees. Ordinary homology of G alone omits the induced vertex partition. [HUNCH] No sheaf and no cohomological existence theorem forcing that partition has been constructed here.

[LIT, EllisMonaghanMoffatt2013] The Penrose polynomial has embedded-graph state sums, coloring evaluations and twisted-dual descriptions; its cubic plane specialization counts Tait colorings. [PROVED-HERE] Two color classes of a Tait coloring give a 2-factor, possibly with several cycles. Counting Tait colorings alone loses Hamiltonian connectivity. None of O, L, C uses this polynomial.

[HUNCH] Temperley-Lieb and planar algebras can record noncrossing loops, but an ordinary fixed scalar per closed loop loses the circulation-dependent factor in Z. Region labels or a connection would have to be added and gluing proved. No planar algebra or TQFT satisfying those axioms has been constructed here. A finite planar tensor contraction is not by itself a TQFT.

[LIT, KrajewskiMoffattTanasa2017] Minor-system Hopf-algebra constructions unify Tutte-type deletion/contraction invariants. [HUNCH] Z has no established deletion/contraction recurrence or multiplicativity on rooted decorated objects, so no Tutte-Grothendieck universal property currently applies. A category or operad of rooted triangulations could record the boundary prescriptions of O Section 7, L Appendix C and C Section 7.3. Their explicit gluing lemma is the proved content; the categorical language has supplied no stronger theorem.

[PROVED-HERE] Positive spokes are discrete Stokes theory. Antisymmetric edge weights are a 1-cochain, with face circulation its coboundary. A dual path from each bounded face to the exterior realizes positive face values. Summing face boundaries inside a counterclockwise simple cycle gives positive circulation. This is not a tension: a tension has zero circulation around cycles. Flow/tension duality explains the linear construction; the disk identity's +/-3 is separate combinatorial information.

[HUNCH] The Lee-Yang comparison should be limited to a zero-order question. O proves a local order of vanishing for each union group and alignment in the smallest degree. It proves no global zero locus, circle theorem or thermodynamic limit for Z. A valuation or lowest-Taylor-degree description is exact; a Lee-Yang theorem would be a new obligation.

## Induced forests, feedback sets and Yutsis graphs

[PROVED-HERE] Either class of a two-induced-tree partition is a feedback vertex set. The larger class has at least half the vertices and is an induced forest. Finding one such large forest is weaker: the complement need not be connected or acyclic. The Eulerian-triangulation statement therefore implies a half-size induced forest for that class, not for every planar graph.

[LIT, Jung2026 and Makarov2026, PREPRINT CLAIMS] Current sources change the usual Albertson-Berman discussion. Two checked August 2026 preprints report counterexamples to the general n/2 induced-forest conjecture. Makarov reports 39 vertices and maximum induced forest 19; Jung reports 31 vertices and maximum 15, extended to a 15/31 family. PDFs are included. I did not audit the proofs or rerun the ancillary verifier. Do not call the general conjecture an established theorem or an unqualified still-open problem. These reports concern general triangulations, not a refutation of this task's Eulerian restriction.

[LIT, Ghent Yutsis project] A cubic Yutsis graph partitions into two vertex-induced trees of equal order. [PROVED-HERE] For a tree class of size a with b crossing edges, cubicity gives 3a=2(a-1)+b, hence b=a+2. The two classes therefore have equal size. This partitions vertices of a CUBIC graph G. The Barnette formulation partitions vertices of its TRIANGULATION T, whose degrees need not be three. The shared words “two induced trees” do not make these certificates equivalent. None of O, L, C uses Yutsis theory.

## Assessment

[HUNCH] The strongest concrete leads are the exact determinant coefficient, simultaneous degree realization, and a pair-level height or orientation model. The clock theorem already answers a different connectivity question. Pfaffian and Floer methods need explicit maps retaining the pair and forest constraints. Test those maps on small triangulations before building a broader formalism.
