# The proof as I understood it after one cold read (written from memory, nothing looked up)

**Goal.** G cubic, bipartite, planar, 3-connected; given an edge e, find a Hamiltonian cycle avoiding e. Work in the dual T, a simple sphere triangulation whose faces are properly 2-coloured black and white (black faces = one colour class of G's vertices). A Hamiltonian cycle of G is the complement of a perfect matching M whose complement is connected. M picks one side of every triangle of T. By tree/cotree duality (Lemma 2), the complement is connected iff the picked sides form a forest in T. So: find a forest P in T with exactly one side of each triangle. Stronger and better for induction: split V(T) into two *induced* trees; then no triangle is monochromatic and each triangle has exactly one edge inside a class, so P = edges inside the classes. Prescribe the pattern on one face t0: the endpoints of a chosen side e0 together, the third corner apart (this is what lets pieces be glued).

**Set-up.** Make t0 the outer face and black; its corners are the roots. n bounded black faces A, n non-root vertices X (Euler). A *state* is a bijection s: A → X with s(t) a corner of t. Q_s = the side of each t opposite s(t). A *pair* (r, s) = two states differing at every face. Then orienting each Q_s edge out of r(t) gives outdegree 1 at non-roots, 0 at roots, so each component of Q_s is a tree with one root or a unicycle with no root; if Q_s is a forest, three trees, one per root.

**Step 1: a pair exists** when T has no separating triangle. Max-flow with capacity 2 on both sides gives a 2-regular spanning subgraph of the incidence graph, which splits into two disjoint perfect matchings. The cut condition reduces to an edge-counting inequality e(U) − b(U) ≤ 2|U| − 4 for vertex sets U containing the roots, proved component by component using that triangular faces of an induced component are faces of T (no separating triangle) and a ±1 side count in the larger regions. I could not reproduce the details of the reduction from memory; I remember only the shape.

**Step 2: disk identity.** If r is a state and each black face chooses a directed edge out of r(t), then any directed cycle of chosen edges encloses one more white than black face; equivalently its length h and the number ℓ of edges with black inside satisfy h − 2ℓ = 3, so the δ-sum (±1 by side of black face) is ±3 by orientation. The key input is B = I + ℓ (the state matches interior black faces to interior vertices and the ℓ boundary tails).

**Step 3: signed count.** For a pair, D(r, s) = edges r(t) → s(t) is a disjoint union of directed cycles covering X, each odd. J = #cw − #ccw, sign ε = (−1)^{(n+J)/2}. Involution 1: if Q_s has a cycle, reverse the first one by changing r there (s fixed); J changes by ±2, sign flips. So Σ ε F(s) over all pairs equals the same sum over forest pairs, for any function F of s. Unweighted (F = 1) the sum is zero (reversing a D-cycle also flips the sign), so F must detect orientation of D-cycles. Heights: for each white face w, a path γ_w to t0, h_s(w) = signed number of spokes (centre → s(t)) crossing γ_w. Reversing a D-cycle C from cw to ccw adds 1 to the height of each white face inside C. Take m = minimum number of cycles of any D, a minimiser D*, a white face w_i inside each cycle of D* (exists by Step 2), F = ∏ h_s(w_i). Grouping by undirected D, inclusion–exclusion kills every group with more than m cycles and gives, for groups with m cycles, a common sign times the number of bijections cycles ↔ w_i with w_i inside its cycle; D* contributes at least 1. So the sum is nonzero, and some pair has Q_s a forest.

**Step 4: recovery.** Take that pair. Spoke graph H (centres plus spokes, including the outer centre). Delete the spokes to s(t): what remains is a subdivided Q_s plus the three outer spokes, a spanning tree of H since the roots are in different components. Its dual is a tree on the white faces. Root it at the white face y0 across e0. Each white face other than y0 takes the side of T corresponding to its parent edge (a side of some t through s(t)); add e0. This gives one side of every face. The selected set minus e0, directed out of s(t), has outdegree ≤ 1; a cycle in it would contradict the disk identity via a black/white pairing count; so it is a forest with three rooted trees, and e0 joins two. The complementary cycle is Hamiltonian; its two sides are the two induced trees, with the prescribed pattern.

**Step 5: gluing.** If T has a separating triangle, cut along it, cap both sides (the cap gets the colour opposite the three faces next to it), solve the outer piece with the original prescription, read off which side of the cap is monochromatic, prescribe that on the inner piece, rename, and glue: the trees meet in a vertex or an edge, so the unions are trees; induced because nothing crosses the triangle.
