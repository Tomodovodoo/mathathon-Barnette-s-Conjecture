# Area circulation used in Figure 9

PROVED-HERE. For the straight-line spoke drawing used here, put a(t,v) = det(c_t,v)/2, where c_t is the black-face centre and v is its corner, with coordinates in the fixed oriented plane. Give the reverse traversal the negative weight. The weight around a simple counterclockwise spoke polygon equals its positive enclosed area.

Proof. List the polygon vertices in traversal order as p_0,...,p_{m-1}, with p_m=p_0. On each edge the assigned weight is det(p_i,p_{i+1})/2. For a centre-to-corner edge this is the definition; for a corner-to-centre edge it follows from antisymmetry of the determinant. Summing gives one half of the sum of x_i y_{i+1} - y_i x_{i+1}. To see that this is the signed area without assuming convexity, triangulate the interior of the simple polygon. Every internal oriented edge occurs in two triangles with opposite directions and cancels. For an oriented triangle the same expression equals half the determinant of its two edge vectors, its signed area. All triangles oriented counterclockwise have positive area. Their sum is the polygon's positive enclosed area. This proves the claim.

For a detour u to c_t to v, its weight is a(t,v)-a(t,u). Summing around a directed D cycle gives L_+ - L_-. Thus a simple counterclockwise detour has positive difference.
