# A history of Barnette's conjecture, 1884 to November 2026

Citations in square brackets are BibTeX keys in `bibliography.bib`. Every claim is `LIT` unless tagged otherwise. Where I relied on a secondary source because the primary one is paywalled or offline, the sentence says so. Row numbers like (B3) point to `RESULTS-TABLE.md`.

## 1. Three conjectures about cubic polyhedra

The story starts with the four colour problem. In 1884 Tait conjectured that every cubic 3-connected planar graph has a Hamiltonian cycle [tait1884]. A Hamiltonian cycle in a cubic graph has even length, so its edges 2-colour alternately and the remaining perfect matching gets the third colour. That 3-edge-colouring is equivalent to a 4-colouring of the faces. Tait's conjecture would have proved the four colour theorem (Holton and Aldred tell this argument in their 1999 survey [holton-aldred1999]).

Tait's conjecture lasted 62 years. Tutte disproved it in 1946 with a 46-vertex cubic 3-connected planar graph built from three copies of a gadget that forces an edge [tutte1946]. Grinberg found an arithmetic way to prove non-Hamiltonicity in 1968: if a plane graph has a Hamiltonian cycle, then sum over i of (i-2)(f_i' - f_i'') = 0, where f_i' and f_i'' count i-gonal faces inside and outside the cycle [grinberg1968]. Graphs whose face sizes make that equation impossible modulo some number are non-Hamiltonian. The smallest counterexamples to Tait were settled by Holton and McKay in 1988. There are exactly six on 38 vertices, among them the Barnette-Bosák-Lederberg graph, and none smaller [holton-mckay1988]. So every Barnette graph on at most 36 vertices is Hamiltonian for free.

All known counterexamples to Tait are non-bipartite. Tutte asked about the other side. In 1971 he wrote that "it is tempting to conjecture that every 3-connected bipartite cubic graph is hamiltonian" [tutte1971], quoted in [bgm2021]. Horton answered with a 96-vertex counterexample, published in Bondy and Murty's 1976 textbook, and a 92-vertex one in 1982 [horton1982]. All these counterexamples are non-planar. A long race for the smallest counterexample followed: Ellingham found 78 vertices, Ellingham and Horton 54 [ellingham-horton1983], Georges 50 [georges1989]. Kelmans had found 50-vertex examples earlier, in 1986, in a Soviet collection that circulated little in the West [kelmans1994], [kelmans1988]. Hertel tells this priority story in his survey [hertel2005]. Brinkmann, Goedgebeur and McKay finally proved in 2022 that the Georges-Kelmans graph is the smallest non-Hamiltonian 3-connected cubic bipartite graph. They also built non-Hamiltonian cyclically 5-connected bipartite cubic graphs, the smallest on 300 vertices, and found no non-Hamiltonian example of genus below 4 in a non-exhaustive search [bgm2021].

## 2. Barnette's conjecture, 1969

Barnette put the two failures together. Counterexamples to Tait are non-bipartite; counterexamples to Tutte are non-planar. So perhaps a graph that is cubic, 3-connected, planar and bipartite is always Hamiltonian. The conjecture appeared as Problem 5 in the proceedings of the Third Waterloo Conference on Combinatorics (May 1968), edited by Tutte and published in 1969, page 343 [barnette1969]. The OpenAI paper and the constructive paper attribute the recording of the problem to Grünbaum. Other sources cite it as "D. Barnette, Conjecture 5". I could not see the page itself. Grünbaum repeated the conjecture in his 1970 Bulletin survey "Polytopes, graphs, and complexes" [grunbaum1970].

In polytope language the conjecture says that every simple 3-polytope whose faces all have an even number of sides has a Hamiltonian graph. In dual language it says that every simple Eulerian plane triangulation has a Hamiltonian dual (see `EQUIVALENTS.md`, entries 1 and 2).

Barnette also has a second, independent Hamiltonicity conjecture, sometimes called the Barnette-Goodey conjecture: cubic 3-connected planar graphs with all faces of size at most 6 are Hamiltonian. Bipartiteness is not assumed. This class contains the fullerenes [holton-aldred1999], [kardos2020]. Kardoš notes that Goodey also stated it informally, which is why it carries both names [kardos2020].

Each hypothesis of Barnette's conjecture is needed. Tutte's graph shows bipartiteness is needed, Horton's graph shows planarity is, and Kirkman's planar 3-connected bipartite non-Hamiltonian graph (with three degree-4 vertices) shows cubicity is [hertel2005]. For 3-connectivity there is a complexity result: Akiyama, Nishizeki and Saito proved in 1980 that Hamiltonicity is NP-complete for cubic bipartite planar 2-connected graphs [ans1980]. Florek and Bagheri Gh. et al. cite this paper as "Takanori, Takao and Nobuji", using the authors' given names [florek2020], [bagheri2021]. Schnieders gives an explicit connected cubic bipartite planar non-Hamiltonian graph whose faces all have size at most 10 [schnieders2025].

## 3. The first partial results, 1971 to 1985

Two ideas from the early 1970s shaped everything after.

The first is Stein's reformulation. In "B-sets and planar maps" (1971) Stein proved that a cubic sphere map is Hamiltonian iff its regions can be split into two classes, neither containing a cycle of regions of length at least three [stein1971]. In the dual this says the vertex set of the triangulation splits into two sets that each induce a forest. Counting edges shows the forests must be trees (APSW Lemma 5 [apsw2016]). This "two induced trees" form is the one the OpenAI proof finally proves. Stein's paper also corrects a false claim about Hamiltonian circuits in an earlier reference.

The second is Goodey's theorem. In 1975 Goodey proved that Barnette graphs whose faces are all quadrilaterals and hexagons are Hamiltonian, and that every edge shared by a quadrilateral and a hexagon lies on a Hamiltonian cycle [goodey1975]. Hertel also credits him with the case of 7 quadrilaterals, one octagon and any number of hexagons [hertel2005]. In 1977 Goodey treated the non-bipartite case of triangles and hexagons [goodey1977]. Goodey's proofs go by induction on the number of faces, reducing local configurations. This is the template that most later partial results follow. The paper is paywalled and I did not see it; the description of the method comes from Hertel.

Then came computers. Holton, Manvel and McKay showed in 1985 that every Barnette graph on at most 64 vertices is Hamiltonian [hmm1985]. Their method has two parts, and both mattered later. First, every Barnette graph can be generated from the cube by two operations, a "cube-expansion" that replaces a vertex by a cube-like 7-vertex gadget and a "C4-expansion" that subdivides two edges at odd distance on a face and joins the new vertices (description from Bekos et al. [bekos2025]). Second, they verify a stronger property, one Hamiltonian cycle through x and avoiding y for pairs of edges, on small graphs by computer, and then lift it to Hamiltonicity of somewhat larger graphs by a theorem. Holton and Aldred's 1999 survey summed up the state of play: "Very little progress has been made on this problem" [holton-aldred1999].

## 4. Kelmans and the strengthening programme, 1986 to 2005

Kelmans' 1986 paper did more than find small counterexamples to Tutte's conjecture. It also proved that Barnette's conjecture is equivalent to stronger statements [kelmans1994]. Every Barnette graph would be (x+y-)-Hamiltonian for any two edges x, y on a common face. And it suffices to prove the conjecture for cyclically 4-edge-connected Barnette graphs, which in the dual means triangulations without separating triangles. The proofs build "four-poles" by cutting two edges of a bad graph and glue copies of them into a non-Hamiltonian Barnette graph. These results stayed obscure. Hertel's 2005 survey [hertel2005] made a point of reviving them, and in 2016 Alt, Payne, Schmidt and Wood published an exposition of Kelmans' proofs, saying that the originals "contained very few details" and that some details were still left to the reader [apsw2016].

Meanwhile the same equivalences were rediscovered in Toronto. Hertel's 2004 master's thesis proved that Barnette's conjecture is equivalent to "every edge lies on a Hamiltonian cycle". Braverman, in a personal communication to Hertel, extended this to "any two edges of a face lie on a Hamiltonian cycle" and hence to "every edge is avoided by a Hamiltonian cycle". Hertel's 2005 survey adds the x-y-z form: for any path of length 3 on a face, some Hamiltonian cycle uses the middle edge and avoids the two end edges. Further corollaries follow: any path of length 3 lies on a Hamiltonian cycle (P4-Hamiltonicity), and a Barnette graph with a quadrilateral has at least six Hamiltonian cycles [hertel2005]. The explicit motive was Tutte's own strategy against Tait. Tutte had strengthened Tait's conjecture until a counterexample became visible [hertel2005]. Here the strengthenings never produced one.

Computation kept pace. Aldred, Brinkmann and McKay announced in 2000 that all Barnette graphs up to 84 vertices are Hamiltonian, after about three years of CPU time. For n ≤ 60, and n ≤ 64 without a 4-face adjacent to two others, they found for every ordered pair of edges x, y a Hamiltonian cycle through x avoiding y (Mohar's problem page [mohar-problem]). Hertel predicted in 2005 that "the days of computers being very helpful with Barnette's Conjecture may be drawing to a close" [hertel2005]. Fowler circulated an unpublished list of subgraphs that cannot occur in a minimal counterexample in 2001, known to me only through Hertel.

Jensen and Toft's book on graph colouring (1995, p. 45) recorded a colouring equivalent: Barnette's conjecture holds iff every 3-colourable planar graph splits into two induced forests. The proof combines Król's characterisation of 3-colourable planar graphs with Stein [harant2013]. Hertel's survey misstates this as a property of the Barnette graph itself and flags in brackets that it cannot be right as stated. The correct statement is about the dual (EQUIVALENTS.md, entry 5).

## 5. Spanning trees of faces and complexity, 2006 to 2022

In 2006 Feder and Subi posted "On Barnette's conjecture" on ECCC [feder-subi2006]. They generalised Goodey's theorem. If the faces of a Barnette graph are properly 3-coloured and two of the three colour classes contain only quadrilaterals and hexagons, the graph is Hamiltonian. They also proved a non-bipartite variant. Their method contracts the faces of the third colour to vertices and looks for a "proper quasi spanning tree of faces" in the reduced graph. That is a set of faces whose vertex-face incidence graph is a tree, so that walking around it traces a Hamiltonian cycle. When the remaining faces have 2 or 3 sides this is a spanning tree parity problem and is polynomial. With 4-sided or arbitrary faces it is NP-complete. They also proved that if Barnette's conjecture is false, Hamiltonicity in the class becomes NP-complete. The paper was never published as such. Its graph-theoretic part became Bagheri Gh., Feder, Fleischner and Subi in J. Graph Theory (2021) [bagheri2021] and its algorithmic part became Fundamenta Informaticae (2022) [bagheri2022]. The 2021 paper proves Hamiltonicity of the leapfrog extension of every cyclically 4-edge-connected planar cubic bipartite graph. It also shows that every 4-connected Eulerian triangulation has a quasi spanning tree of faces, and proves a Goodey-type theorem with parity conditions on the separating triangles of the reduced graph [bagheri2021]. These papers also recall Fleischner's A-trail equivalent: Barnette's conjecture holds iff every simple 3-connected Eulerian triangulation has an A-trail [bagheri2021].

Florek worked in the dual throughout. His 2010 paper shows that Barnette graphs with a 2-factor of facial 4-cycles are Hamiltonian [florek2010] (as cited in [florek2025]). Later papers give sufficient conditions through the degree-at-least-6 vertices of the colour classes of the triangulation [florek2016], count Hamiltonian cycles [florek2020], and prove Hamiltonicity when no face has more than four neighbours of size at least six [florek2025]. Lu (2011) reformulated the conjecture through "2-tree colourings" of even triangulations and proved some configurations reducible [lu2011]; I could only read the abstract. Harant (2013) proved a striking equivalence. Barnette's conjecture holds iff Barnette graphs have paths of linear length [harant2013]. A single non-Hamiltonian Barnette graph would make the longest cycles in the class sublinear.

Alt, Payne, Schmidt and Wood (arXiv 2013, journal 2016) proved a new sufficient condition in the dual. Colour the vertices of the triangulation blue and red so that blue vertices hit every face, there is no blue cycle, and every red cycle has a vertex of degree at most 4. Then the dual is Hamiltonian. They also proved a limitation theorem. For every k there is a properly 3-coloured Eulerian triangulation in which every permeating subtree contains at least k vertices of each colour class and misses at least k of each. So no construction that takes or leaves whole colour classes can prove the conjecture [apsw2016].

Gorsky, Steiner and Wiederrecht (arXiv 2022, journal 2023) translated the conjecture into matching theory [gsw2023]. For cubic 3-connected bipartite graphs, braces are exactly the cyclically 4-connected ones. Barnette's conjecture is equivalent to Hamiltonicity of all cubic planar braces and, more surprisingly, to Hamiltonicity of all cubic 3-connected Pfaffian bipartite graphs, planar or not. All of these are equivalent to P4-Hamiltonicity of those classes (their Theorem 4.16). On the computational side, Brinkmann, Goedgebeur and McKay pushed the verification to 90 vertices, with every edge on a Hamiltonian cycle up to 78 vertices and H+- for all edge pairs up to 66 vertices, using about 42 CPU years [bgm2021].

## 6. The approach to 2026

The second Barnette conjecture fell first. Kardoš proved in a computer-assisted paper (arXiv 2014, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 2020) that cubic 3-connected planar graphs with faces of size at most 6 are Hamiltonian. The proof uses Marušič's idea of decomposing into an induced forest and a stable set, together with nanotube structure. In particular all fullerenes are Hamiltonian [kardos2020]. Shao and Wu (2025) relaxed 3-connectivity to 2-connectivity and showed the face bound 6 is tight [shao-wu2025].

For Barnette's conjecture itself, 2025 brought two new partial results. Bekos, Kaufmann and Pfister showed every n-vertex Barnette graph has a subhamiltonian cycle with at least 5n/6 edges. Equivalently there is a 2-page book embedding with that many spine edges, and n would settle the conjecture [bekos2025]. They point out that in the Holton-Manvel-McKay generation, cube-expansions preserve Hamiltonicity, so the difficulty is in C4-expansions. Schnieders proved in August 2025 that cubic bipartite planar graphs with all faces of size at most 8 are Hamiltonian, even without 3-connectivity. The proof is a minimal-counterexample argument with an Euler count (f4 = 6 + f8) and 339,068,624 computer-checked substitution cases. The bound 8 is sharp for connected graphs [schnieders2025]. His closing section asks whether Hamiltonicity of Barnette graphs is "a local property" and names the obstacles: case explosion, unbounded faces that no finite neighbourhood covers, and the need to use 3-connectivity once faces reach size 10 [schnieders2025].

Several claimed full proofs appeared along the way. None was accepted. Cahit posted an "algorithmic proof" draft in 2009 [cahit2009], Clarke posted an inductive proof in 2013 and withdrew it in April 2014 "for further review" [clarke2013], and Shah published a claimed proof in the International Journal of Mathematical Combinatorics in 2014 [shah2014]. Every later paper I read still calls the conjecture open, and none cites these as proofs. I did not audit them.

## 7. The OpenAI proof and its aftermath (September to November 2026)

The manuscript "Paired states and Hamiltonian cycles in cubic bipartite planar graphs", credited to OpenAI and dated 24 September 2026, proves Barnette's conjecture [openai2026]. It is family 180 of the OpenAI mathematics release, a public GitHub repository whose initial commit is dated 6 October 2026. The repository README describes the 721 manuscripts as produced "by an unnamed internal OpenAI model", calls them "raw research data, rather than polished academic papers", and says that "some of the unformalized results could have issues" [openai-math-repo]. The release history records withdrawals of other manuscripts on 7 October 2026 after a sign error. Nothing in it concerns family 180 [openai-math-repo]. The PDF in our `papers/` folder is byte-identical to the one in the repository on 14 November 2026 (`COMPUTED`: `cmp` against a fresh download).

The proof works in the dual Eulerian triangulation and proves the stronger statement that the vertices split into two induced trees with a prescribed facial edge inside one tree. In primal terms, there is a Hamiltonian cycle avoiding any prescribed edge, which is the old H- equivalent of Hertel, Braverman and Kelmans. The new ingredients are Tutte's face-vertex matchings ("states") from his theory of trinity [tutte1975], with Hine and Kálmán's modern treatment [hine-kalman2018], pairs of disjoint states, a "disk identity", and a complex exponential sum over pairs. In that sum all pairs with a cyclic `Q_s` cancel, while a second regrouping by cycles shows the sum is nonzero. Separating triangles are handled by gluing, as in Kelmans' reduction. Through Gorsky-Steiner-Wiederrecht the paper also gets P4-Hamiltonicity for cubic 3-connected Pfaffian bipartite graphs.

The repository includes a Lean formalisation: `lean/docs/180.md` says "The formalization proves this statement for every such graph". The comparator challenge `lean/ComparatorChallenges/BarnetteHamiltonian.lean` states the theorem with planarity given by a topological crossing-free embedding into ℝ², and the solution module `OAI.Combinatorics.Hamiltonian.Main` is 31 files, about 8,900 lines, importing only Mathlib. The permitted axioms are `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`. I downloaded the files and found no `sorry`, `admit` or `axiom` by grep, but I did not build them (`COMPUTED`, grep only; see REPORT.md). The file names (`SquareGrid`, `Corridors`, `MinorModel`, `ExactDual`) suggest that a large part of the formal proof turns a topological embedding into a combinatorial dual. That is a different problem from the one the paper discusses (`HUNCH`). On Hacker News, the commenter jboggan wrote around the release that "There's no Lean proof for this one" [hn-2026]. The GitHub API lists the Lean files in the initial commit of 6 October, so either the comment misread the catalogue or the history was rewritten. I cannot tell which.

Reactions were quick. Gil Kalai's blog listed it on 7 October 2026 among the release's results, noting that he had discussed the conjecture earlier [kalai-blog2026]. In the Hacker News thread on OpenAI's announcement, jboggan, who says he worked on the conjecture for 24 years, called the key insight remarkable. He said it was the first time he had seen "complex roots and annihilating terms like this" in graph theory. Another commenter, thomasahle, called the argument "quite novel" while tracing the cancellation idea to loop-model physics [hn-2026]. These are informal comments, not reviews.

Expositions followed. On 18 October 2026 an unsigned manuscript, "A constructive proof of Barnette's conjecture", was written. Its PDF metadata has no author, and I found no public copy online. It replaces the analytic argument by a signed integer polynomial, a deterministic walk between marked matching pairs (Garsia-Milne style), an exact criterion for when the incidence tree exists (no separating triangle with black inner boundary), and an enumeration algorithm in O(poly(N) 3^(N/4)) time. On 21 October 2026 Zhentao Li posted "A note on the proof of Barnette's conjecture" on arXiv. It works in the primal, through the face-black-vertex incidence graph and a "cycle-breaking" matching, and replaces the exponential sum by two sign-reversing involutions [li2026].

On the formal side, the GitHub user anxkhn opened issue #7144 and pull request #7149 in google-deepmind/formal-conjectures on 11 October 2026 to add the statement in a "research solved" category, without a `formal_proof` attribute. A reviewer asked what evidence should be required before marking it solved, since "citing a manuscript alone does not establish acceptance". Another contributor reported that OpenAI's Lean `main` implies the PR's statement, without checking OpenAI's proof. The PR was still open on 14 November 2026 [formal-conjectures-pr]. Downstream, Hongmin Li's Zenodo preprint (October-November 2026) treats the conjecture as proved and builds exponentially many Barnette graphs with the global H+- property, which for all Barnette graphs remains open. The author states that the work "was developed with AI assistance and has not been independently reviewed by experts" [hongmin-li2026].

What I did not find: any refereed publication of the proof, any published refutation or correction specific to family 180, any Quanta or other press article about this result, or any public statement by the earlier workers (Feder, Fleischner, Florek, Kardoš, McKay, Wood and others) that I could locate. The Wikipedia article, last edited 10 October 2026 when I read it, still listed the conjecture as unsolved. This list is only as good as my searches (see REPORT.md).
